# 01｜专题：先秦——青铜、石鼓与竹简

## 中文

[《大盂鼎》](../works/dayu-ding.md)腹内壁铸铭十九行、二百九十一字，是可核起点。中国国家博物馆[“盂”青铜鼎](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/csp/202008/t20200826_247375.shtml)条目记周康王时期、通高101.9厘米、重153.5千克，1959年入藏。作品页记西周康王二十三年（公元前1003年）、字属金文，铭文记康王册命与殷商酗酒亡国训诫。出土地有郿县礼村与岐山两说，馆方页面不一致；『二十三年』依小盂鼎推定、非鼎铭自书王名，并列待考。

[《史墙盘》](../works/shiqiang-pan.md)内底铸铭十八行、二百八十四字，前半颂文、武、成、康、昭、穆及恭王，后半记微氏六代家史。[史墙盘](https://cssn.cn/skgz/bwyc/202212/t20221228_5574599.shtml)记通高16.2厘米、口径47.3厘米、重12.45千克，现藏宝鸡青铜器博物院；[宝博微课堂｜周原的世家大族——微氏家族（上）](https://www.bjqtm.com/?p=5143)记1976年扶风庄白一号窖藏、墙为微氏第六代史官。作品页记西周恭王时期。微氏高祖封地有异说、『恭王』据世系推定而铭文未书王名，均待考。

[《中山王厝鼎》](../works/zhongshan-wang-cuo-ding.md)外壁刻铭七十七行、四百六十九字。河北博物院[中山王 铁足铜鼎](https://www.hebeimuseum.org.cn/shida/tongding.html)条目记战国、1977年平山县三汲村中山王墓出土、高51.5厘米，称字体修长秀丽，称其铭文为战国铜器中最长者。作品页记书体中山篆，铭文记相邦司马赒伐燕之功与对后嗣告诫，线条尖入尖出。铭文是铸是刻尚无定论；中山三器总字数有1123字与1124字两说，本页只记此鼎469字。

[《石鼓文》](../works/stone-drum-inscriptions.md)是十件鼓形石上的篆书诗篇，涉出行与田猎。故宫[秦石鼓](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234438.html)著录为秦国遗物、确切年代有争议，目录列不同断代；[明拓战国石鼓文册](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228598.html)把原石系于此并录明拓本，故宫[第一百一十三讲：石鼓文及其拓本](https://www.dpm.org.cn/gugongforum/detail/245912.html)讲大观迁徙与1933年疏散。作品页记书体大篆、年代『秦国遗物，确切年代未定』；列入先秦组不取确切年份。

[《郭店楚简》](../works/guodian-chujian.md)1993年出土于荆门郭店村战国楚墓。[央视《简牍探中华》聚焦郭店楚简，探秘“神州第一书”](https://www.jmmuseum.com/Social/detail/id/3967.html)记804枚竹简、书写超13000字、含《老子》抄本与《太一生水》；该页记有字简731枚，馆方另一页作730枚，两说并列、本页不取舍。作品页记楚系简帛书、战国中期，抄《老子》《太一生水》《五行》《缁衣》等十八篇。各篇成书年代不一，本记录只记墓葬时代，属待考。

几件并看，可辨石、竹、笔墨三种载体：石鼓是刻在十件鼓形石上的字，郭店是写在竹简上的字，以笔墨成之；青铜诸器为铸铭，与手写不同。[秦石鼓](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234438.html)与[央视《简牍探中华》聚焦郭店楚简，探秘“神州第一书”](https://www.jmmuseum.com/Social/detail/id/3967.html)给出石与竹的材料线索。本页把铸、刻、写并列只为对照，不替材料排先后。

各页可核项不齐整：字数、行数、尺寸多寡不一，书体名目分作金文、大篆、楚系简帛书。[“盂”青铜鼎](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/csp/202008/t20200826_247375.shtml)记十九行二百九十一字，[史墙盘](https://cssn.cn/skgz/bwyc/202212/t20221228_5574599.shtml)记十八行二百八十四字，[中山王 铁足铜鼎](https://www.hebeimuseum.org.cn/shida/tongding.html)记七十七行四百六十九字，皆以行款为先。本页不把多寡当高下，只把数字作把手；字形方圆、行气整饬一类判断，作品页虽有转述，仍待读者自看。

1. **[《大盂鼎》](../works/dayu-ding.md)**：看腹内壁十九行二百九十一字，先数行款再辨方圆，出土地两说并存。
2. **[《史墙盘》](../works/shiqiang-pan.md)**：看内底十八行二百八十四字，前半颂王、后半记微氏六代，『恭王』为推定。
3. **[《中山王厝鼎》](../works/zhongshan-wang-cuo-ding.md)**：看外壁七十七行四百六十九字，字形修长尖入尖出，铸刻尚无定论。
4. **[《石鼓文》](../works/stone-drum-inscriptions.md)**：看十件鼓形石上的篆书诗篇，断代有争议，先秦组不取确切年份。
5. **[《郭店楚简》](../works/guodian-chujian.md)**：看八百余枚竹简上的楚系笔墨，有字简730与731两说并列不取。

待考：大盂鼎出土地郿县礼村与岐山两说、史墙盘『恭王』据世系推定与微氏封地异说、中山王厝鼎铸刻未定与三器总字数1123／1124两说、郭店有字简730与731两说、石鼓[秦石鼓](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234438.html)条目断代不一。本页的编排按金文、大篆到楚系简帛列出五件作品，是编辑选择，不取确切年份，也不把顺序写成书体演变的因果；来源未记的尺寸、书体与递藏，不补确定答案。 续读：[专题 02](../notes/essay-02-qin-han.md)、[路径 09](../notes/path-01-clerical-turn.md)。

## English

[Da Yu Ding, the Great Yu Cauldron](../works/dayu-ding.md): a cast inscription on the inner belly wall, nineteen lines, 291 characters — checkable start. The National Museum of China's [“盂”青铜鼎](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/csp/202008/t20200826_247375.shtml) entry gives King Kang's reign, height 101.9 cm, weight 153.5 kg, accessioned 1959. The work page: his twenty-third year (1003 BCE), bronze script, recording the royal charge and Yin-Shang's ruin by drunkenness. Findspots Meixian Licun and Qishan differ across museum pages; 'twenty-third year' is inferred from the Small Yu Ding, no royal name in the casting. Both stay open.

[Shi Qiang Pan, the Qiang Basin](../works/shiqiang-pan.md): a family history cast on the inner bottom, eighteen lines, 284 characters — the first half praising Kings Wen, Wu, Cheng, Kang, Zhao, Mu and Gong, the second six Wei generations. [史墙盘](https://cssn.cn/skgz/bwyc/202212/t20221228_5574599.shtml) gives height 16.2 cm, rim 47.3 cm, weight 12.45 kg, Baoji Bronze Ware Museum; [宝博微课堂｜周原的世家大族——微氏家族（上）](https://www.bjqtm.com/?p=5143) gives the 1976 Zhuangbai No. 1 hoard at Fufeng, Qiang the sixth-generation Wei scribe. The work page: Western Zhou, King Gong. The founder's fief differs; 'King Gong' inferred from genealogy, no royal name in the inscription — both open.

[Ding of King Cuo of Zhongshan](../works/zhongshan-wang-cuo-ding.md): seventy-seven lines, 469 characters on the outer wall. The Hebei Museum [中山王 铁足铜鼎](https://www.hebeimuseum.org.cn/shida/tongding.html) entry gives Warring States, 1977 digging from the Zhongshan king's tomb at Sanji Village, Pingshan, height 51.5 cm, calling the script slender and elegant and the inscription the longest among Warring States bronzes. The work page: Zhongshan seal script, recording Chancellor Sima Zhou's Yan campaign and warnings to heirs, pointed strokes. Cast or engraved unsettled; the three vessels total 1123 or 1124 characters, this page noting only this ding's 469.

[Stone Drum Inscriptions](../works/stone-drum-inscriptions.md): seal-script poems on ten drum-shaped stones, their content travel and hunting. The Palace Museum's [秦石鼓](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234438.html) catalogues them as relics of the Qin state, exact date disputed, listing different datings; [明拓战国石鼓文册](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228598.html) locates the original stones here and records a Ming rubbing, while [第一百一十三讲：石鼓文及其拓本](https://www.dpm.org.cn/gugongforum/detail/245912.html) treats the Daguan transfer and 1933 evacuation. The work page: large seal script, 'a Qin relic, exact date undetermined'; the pre-Qin group adopts no exact year.

[Guodian Chu Slips](../works/guodian-chujian.md): unearthed 1993 from a Warring States Chu tomb at Guodian Village, Jingmen. [央视《简牍探中华》聚焦郭店楚简，探秘“神州第一书”](https://www.jmmuseum.com/Social/detail/id/3967.html) records 804 slips, over 13,000 characters, a Laozi (《老子》) manuscript and the Taiyi Shengshui (《太一生水》); that page gives 731 inscribed slips, another museum page 730, both kept side by side. The work page: Chu bamboo-and-silk writing, mid Warring States, eighteen texts including Laozi, Taiyi Shengshui, Wuxing (《五行》) and Ziyi (《缁衣》). Text dates differ; this record notes only the tomb period — open.

Set together, three supports appear here — stone, bamboo, brush-and-ink: the Stone Drums are characters cut into ten drum-shaped stones, the Guodian slips characters written on bamboo, formed with brush and ink; the bronzes instead carry cast inscriptions, unlike handwriting. [秦石鼓](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234438.html) and [央视《简牍探中华》聚焦郭店楚简，探秘“神州第一书”](https://www.jmmuseum.com/Social/detail/id/3967.html) give the material clues for stone and bamboo respectively. This page sets casting, cutting and writing side by side for comparison only, not a sequence of materials.

Checkable items are not uniform: counts, lines and dimensions vary; script labels divide into bronze writing, large seal script and Chu bamboo-and-silk writing. [“盂”青铜鼎](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/zp/zpml/csp/202008/t20200826_247375.shtml) records nineteen lines, 291 characters, [史墙盘](https://cssn.cn/skgz/bwyc/202212/t20221228_5574599.shtml) eighteen lines, 284, [中山王 铁足铜鼎](https://www.hebeimuseum.org.cn/shida/tongding.html) seventy-seven lines, 469, all taking line layout first. This page makes no ranking from more or fewer, taking numbers as handles into each work; judgments of square-and-round forms or ordered spacing, though paraphrased on the work pages, are left to the reader.

1. **[Da Yu Ding, the Great Yu Cauldron](../works/dayu-ding.md)**: Look at nineteen lines, 291 characters on the inner belly wall; count layout before forms, two findspots kept.
2. **[Shi Qiang Pan, the Qiang Basin](../works/shiqiang-pan.md)**: Look at eighteen lines, 284 characters on the inner bottom: praise of kings, six Wei generations; 'Gong' inferred.
3. **[Ding of King Cuo of Zhongshan](../works/zhongshan-wang-cuo-ding.md)**: Look at seventy-seven lines, 469 characters, slender pointed forms; cast or engraved unsettled.
4. **[Stone Drum Inscriptions](../works/stone-drum-inscriptions.md)**: Look at seal-script poems on ten drum-shaped stones, dating disputed; the pre-Qin group takes no exact year.
5. **[Guodian Chu Slips](../works/guodian-chujian.md)**: Look at Chu brush-and-ink on eight hundred odd slips; 730 and 731 inscribed slips kept side by side.

Open points stay together: the Da Yu Ding's two findspots, Meixian Licun and Qishan; the Shi Qiang Pan's 'King Gong' inferred from genealogy and differing Wei fief accounts; the Zhongshan ding's unsettled cast-or-engraved and totals 1123 and 1124; the 730 and 731 inscribed Guodian slips; the differing datings in the [秦石鼓](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234438.html) catalogue. This page arranges the five works in a reading order from bronze writing to large seal script to Chu bamboo-and-silk writing — an editorial choice, no exact year, not a causal chain of script change.

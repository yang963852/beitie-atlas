# 诸上座帖

## 背景

### 中文

纸本手卷，草书，九十二行，纵三十三厘米，横七百二十九点五厘米。内容是黄庭坚为友人李任道录写的五代金陵僧人文益《语录》，全篇禅语。署款「山谷老人书」，「书」字上钤「山谷道人」朱文方玺；后纸有明吴宽、清梁清标题跋各一段。[故宫博物院，黄庭坚草书诸上座帖卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228218.html)

馆方说此卷师法怀素狂草，「笔意纵横，气势苍浑雄伟，字法奇宕，如马脱缰，无所拘束」。卷中所引黄庭坚《山谷自论》自述学草经过：「余学草书三十余年，初以周越为师，故二十年抖擞俗气不脱，晚得苏才翁、子美书观之，乃得古人笔意。其后又得张长史、僧怀素、高闲墨迹，乃窥笔法之妙。」在《语录》之后，他又用大字行楷写了一段自识，结字内紧外松，出笔长而遒劲；一卷之内草书与行楷并见，馆方称为「晚年杰作」。[故宫博物院，黄庭坚草书诸上座帖卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228218.html)

此帖初藏南宋高宗内府，后归贾似道；明代递藏于李应祯、华夏、周亮工处；清初藏孙承泽砚山斋，后归王鸿绪，乾隆时收入内府；民国初流出宫外，为张伯驹所得，后捐献国家，现藏故宫博物院。都穆《寓意编》、华夏《真赏斋赋注》、文嘉《钤山堂书画记》、张丑《清河书画舫》《清河见闻表》、卞永誉《式古堂书画汇考》、孙承泽《庚子消夏记》、《石渠宝笈初编》等书著录。[故宫博物院，黄庭坚草书诸上座帖卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228218.html)

### English

Ink on paper, a handscroll in cursive script, ninety-two lines, 33 by 729.5 cm. It is Huang Tingjian’s transcription for his friend Li Rendao of the recorded sayings of Wenyi, a monk of Jinling in the Five Dynasties; the whole text is Chan Buddhist speech. It is signed “written by the old man of Shangu”, with a red square seal reading “Shangu daoren” over the character for “written”; two colophons follow, by Wu Kuan of the Ming and Liang Qingbiao of the Qing. [Palace Museum, Huang Tingjian’s Discourse to the Venerable Ones](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228218.html)

The museum says the scroll follows Huaisu’s wild cursive, with “brush intent running free, momentum vast and powerful, character method extraordinary, like a horse off the rein”. It quotes Huang’s own account of learning cursive: “I studied cursive for more than thirty years. At first I took Zhou Yue as my teacher, and for twenty years the vulgar air would not shake off. Late I saw the writing of Su Caiweng and Su Zimei and began to get the ancients’ intention; afterwards I saw the ink of Zhang Changshi, the monk Huaisu and Gao Xian, and then I peeped into the wonder of brush method.” After the sayings he added a note of his own in large running-regular script, tight within the character and loose outside it, with long, forceful exits; one scroll holds both cursive and running-regular writing, and the museum calls it a late masterpiece. [Palace Museum, Huang Tingjian’s Discourse to the Venerable Ones](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228218.html)

The scroll first belonged to the Southern Song court of Emperor Gaozong, then to Jia Sidao; in the Ming it passed through Li Yingzhen, Hua Xia and Zhou Lianggong; in the early Qing it was kept in Sun Chengze’s Yanshan Studio, later with Wang Hongxu, and entered the Qianlong palace collection. In the early Republic it left the palace and was acquired by Zhang Boju, who later donated it to the state; it is now in the Palace Museum in Beijing. It is recorded in Du Mu’s Yuyi bian, Hua Xia’s Zhenshang zhai fuzhu, Wen Jia’s Qianshan tang shuhua ji, Zhang Chou’s Qinghe shuhua fang and Qinghe jianwen biao, Bian Yongyu’s Shigutang shuhua huikao, Sun Chengze’s Gengzi xiaoxia ji and the first series of the Shiqu baoji. [Palace Museum, Huang Tingjian’s Discourse to the Venerable Ones](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228218.html)

## 待考

### 中文

- 书写年份不详，馆方只称「晚年杰作」，不系年。
- 九十二行与尺寸依馆方藏品页；释文中「以下点去十六字」，点去后的字数未计。
- 卷长七米余，现存装裱是否原装，馆方页未说明。

### English

- The year of writing is unknown; the museum calls it a late masterpiece without dating it.
- The line count and measurements follow the museum’s catalogue page. The transcription notes sixteen characters struck out; the remaining count is not given.
- The scroll is over seven metres long, and the museum page does not say whether the present mounting is original.

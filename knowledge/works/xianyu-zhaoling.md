# 行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷

## 背景

### 中文

故宫博物院藏本为纸本手卷，纵32厘米，横342厘米，文物号新00145380，馆方定为元代鲜于枢行书。所书为杜甫五言《行次昭陵诗》；杜甫是诗作者，鲜于枢是书写者。馆方释文如下：

> 舊俗疲庸主，群雄問獨夫。<br>
> 譏歸龍鳳質，威定虎狼都。<br>
> 天屬尊堯典，神功協禹謨。<br>
> 風雲隨絕足，日月繼高衢。<br>
> 文物多師古，朝廷半老儒。<br>
> 直詞寧戮辱，賢路不崎嶇。<br>
> 往者災猶降，蒼生喘未蘇。<br>
> 指揮安率土，盪滌撫洪爐。<br>
> 壯士悲陵邑，幽人拜鼎湖。<br>
> 玉衣晨自舉，鐵馬汗長趨。<br>
> 松柏瞻虛殿，塵沙立冥途。<br>
> 寂寞開國日，流恨滿山隅。

诗后署：“右工部行次昭陵詩，困學民書。”钤“鲜于”“白几印章”“箕子之裔”“虎林隐吏”“中山后人”印五方。卷末有王祎、宋濂二跋；鉴藏印记有清乾隆、嘉庆、宣统诸玺及梁清标、宋濂、王祎等印多方。[故宫博物院，鲜于枢行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

撰稿人张彬称此卷结体疏朗、笔势雄浑，与鲜于枢个人性情相合，并判为“鲜于枢大字行书的代表作品”。同段引柳贯评语：“公毅然美大夫，面带河朔伟气，每酒酣骜放，挥毫结字，奇态横生，势有不可遏者。”这是馆方转引的柳贯意见，不是本记录对原件的目验。明顾复《平生壮观》卷四、清《石渠宝笈初编》卷三一及《故宫已佚书画目》有著录。[故宫博物院，鲜于枢行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

馆方只定“元”，未记书写年份。鲜于枢生卒为1246—1301年，这是其生卒，不是本卷的制作年代范围。[故宫博物院，鲜于枢行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

### English

The Palace Museum’s paper handscroll is 32 cm high and 342 cm long, under inventory number 新00145380. The museum dates it to the Yuan and attributes the running script to Xianyu Shu. The text is Du Fu’s five-character poem “Stopping at Zhaoling”: Du Fu wrote the poem, while Xianyu wrote the calligraphy. The museum’s transcription is given above. The English rendering below leaves “譏歸龍鳳質” untranslated because the catalogue supplies no reading or commentary for that wording.

> Old customs burdened the mediocre ruler;<br>
> rival chiefs questioned the lone tyrant.<br>
> “譏歸龍鳳質” [catalogue wording retained without translation];<br>
> his might settled the tiger-and-wolf capital.<br>
> As Heaven’s heir he honored Yao’s canon;<br>
> divine deeds matched Yu’s plans.<br>
> Wind and cloud followed his unmatched stride;<br>
> sun and moon continued his lofty road.<br>
> Culture took the ancients as its models;<br>
> half the court were senior scholars.<br>
> Straight words were better than slaughter and disgrace;<br>
> the path to worth was not tortuous.<br>
> In the past, disaster still descended;<br>
> the people’s breath had not revived.<br>
> His command brought peace to the realm;<br>
> his cleansing comforted the vast furnace.<br>
> Strong men mourn the tomb city;<br>
> recluses bow at Lake Dinghu.<br>
> Jade robes rise at dawn;<br>
> iron horses sweat in their long advance.<br>
> Pines and cypresses face the empty hall;<br>
> dust and sand stand on the dark road.<br>
> Lonely was the day the kingdom was founded;<br>
> boundless regret fills the mountain corner.

After the poem is written: “Right, the poem on stopping at Zhaoling by the Minister of Works; written by Kunxue Min.” Five seals follow: “鲜于”, “白几印章”, “箕子之裔”, “虎林隐吏” and “中山后人”. The end carries colophons by Wang Yi and Song Lian. Collector seals include those of the Qianlong, Jiaqing and Xuantong emperors, Liang Qingbiao, Song Lian and Wang Yi.[Palace Museum, Xianyu Shu’s running-script scroll of Du Fu’s poem on stopping at Zhaoling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

The catalogue author, Zhang Bin, describes the structure as open and the brush force as vigorous, matching Xianyu Shu’s temperament, and calls it “a representative work of Xianyu Shu’s large-character running script”. The same passage quotes Liu Guan: “He stood firm as a handsome gentleman, with the great air of the north in his face; whenever he had drunk his fill and gave himself freely, his brush formed characters in strange postures, with a force that could not be stopped.” This is Liu Guan’s opinion as quoted by the museum, not an examination of the object by this record. The scroll is recorded in Gu Fu’s *Pingsheng zhuangguan*, juan 4, the *Shiqu baoji*, first series, juan 31, and the *Gugong yishi shuhua mu*.[Palace Museum, Xianyu Shu’s running-script scroll of Du Fu’s poem on stopping at Zhaoling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

The museum dates the scroll only to the Yuan and gives no writing year. Xianyu Shu’s dates, 1246–1301, are his lifespan, not a production range for this scroll.[Palace Museum, Xianyu Shu’s running-script scroll of Du Fu’s poem on stopping at Zhaoling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

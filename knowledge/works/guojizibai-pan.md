# 虢季子白盘

## 背景

### 中文

西周宣王十二年，虢季子白率军与玁狁战于洛水之阳，斩敌五百、俘虏五十，周王设宴赏赐车马弓矢斧钺，子白作盘铭记其功。盘内底铸铭八行一百一十一字，以四字句为主、修饰用韵，馆方称其为“藏在金文中的诗”，并与《诗·小雅》诸篇互证。字体修长、笔画匀称，书风恬静疏朗，在西周晚期金文中独树一帜；馆方专题指出，由此亦可窥见其后《石鼓文》与秦小篆的源头。习金文者常以它与大盂鼎、散氏盘、毛公鼎对读，看西周金文的多种面貌。

### English

In the twelfth year of King Xuan of Zhou, Guo Ji Zibai fought the Xianyun on the north bank of the Luo river, beheading five hundred and capturing fifty. The king feasted him and rewarded him with chariots, horses, bows, axes, and Zibai cast this pan to record his merit. Eight lines and 111 characters on the inner base are mostly four-character phrases with rhyme, which the museum calls “a poem hidden in bronze writing”, cross-checked against poems in the Minor Odes of the Shijing. The characters are tall and even, the manner quiet and unforced, standing apart in late Western Zhou bronze script; the museum's essay notes that from it one can glimpse the source of the later Stone Drums and Qin small-seal writing. Students of bronze script often read it beside the Da Yu, San Shi and Mao Gong vessels to see the range of Western Zhou writing.

### English

- The museum page records only the Daoguang-era find and the donation by Liu Suzeng. The later chain of custody — Xu Xie taking it to Changzhou, the Taiping commander Chen Kunshu keeping it, Liu Mingchuan finding it there in 1864 — is reported by People's Daily and is a later account; the exact year of excavation is unknown.
- Individual character readings vary slightly among scholars; this record adopts no definitive transcription.

# 急就章

## 背景

### 中文

《急就章》又名《急就篇》，故宫博物院定为西汉史游撰的识字课本。本档案的1370年指[宋克](../people/song-ke.md)书写此卷之年，不是史游撰文之年。[故宫博物院藏品说明](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230117.html)

此卷纸本、章草，纵20.3厘米，横342.5厘米，款署“吴郡宋克书”。自识记“庚戌七月十八日”偶阅佳纸，“遂书皇象急就章”，计十纸、一千九百余字。卷前有姚绶隶书五字引首“宋南宫章草”；后隔水及尾纸有周鼎、孙廷惠、朱之赤、项元汴、宋荦、铁保六家题跋。华宁据自识及全篇面貌定此卷为宋克44岁时的精心临摹之作。

故宫说明称，宋克临习的章草范本传为三国吴皇象所书；这是对范本的传统归名，不是对宋克此卷归属的质疑。该页又引王世贞评宋克《急就章》“结意纯美”，并称其与皇象石本的大小、行模及缺处若一。故宫的小传另记宋克沿袭[赵孟頫](../people/zhao-mengfu.md)、邓文原余绪而自成面目。

### English

The Palace Museum identifies the *Jijiu zhang*, also called *Jijiu pian*, as a Western Han primer by Shi You. The year 1370 in this record dates [Song Ke’s writing of the scroll](../people/song-ke.md), not Shi You’s composition of the text. [Palace Museum catalogue entry](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230117.html)

The scroll is on paper in zhangcao, 20.3 cm high and 342.5 cm long, and signed “Wu jun Song Ke shu.” Song’s self-note records that on the eighteenth day of the seventh month of Gengxu he found fine paper and “thereupon wrote Huang Xiang’s *Jijiu zhang*,” using ten sheets and more than 1,900 characters. Yao Shou wrote the five-character clerical-script frontispiece “Song Nangong zhangcao.” Colophons by Zhou Ding, Sun Tinghui, Zhu Zhichi, Xiang Yuanbian, Song Luo and Tie Bao appear on the rear mounting paper and final paper. Hua Ning dates the scroll to Song Ke’s forty-fourth year and describes it as his carefully executed copy.

The museum says that the zhangcao model from which Song studied was traditionally attributed to Huang Xiang of the Three Kingdoms Wu. That attribution concerns the model; it is not an authenticity dispute about Song’s scroll. The page also quotes Wang Shizhen’s praise of the “pure and beautiful” construction of Song’s *Jijiu zhang* and its correspondence with a Huang Xiang stone edition in format and omissions. The museum biography separately places Song’s mature manner after [Zhao Mengfu](../people/zhao-mengfu.md) and Deng Wenyuan.

### English

- The exact dates, places and sequence of the six families’ colophons remain unverified.
- The precise Huang Xiang edition meant by the self-note and museum account, and its relation to the edition Song actually used, remain open.
- This record does not reproduce the full transcription or collate its textual omissions, losses and errors against another edition.

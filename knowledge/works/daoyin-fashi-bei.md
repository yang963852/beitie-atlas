# 道因法师碑

## 背景

### 中文

唐龙朔三年（663）十月镌立，李俨撰文，欧阳通书。螭首龟趺，通高三百一十二厘米；北宋移存西安碑林。[西安碑林博物馆，道因法师碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

碑主道因法师（587—658），隋末避乱益州（今成都）东门外多宝寺；后「追赴京邑，止大慈恩寺，与玄奘法师证译梵本」。碑记其生平。[西安碑林博物馆，道因法师碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

书者欧阳通（625—691）是欧阳询之子。初拜兰台郎，仪凤中迁中书舍人，封渤海公；天授初年转司礼卿判纳言事，二年（691）为相，因反立武承嗣为太子，为酷吏来俊臣陷害而死。他工楷书，得父法而险峻过之，父子齐名，号「大小欧阳」；所留碑版仅有二种，另一种为《泉男生墓志》。明王世贞评此碑：「如病维摩，高格贫士，虽不饶乐，而眉宇间有风霜之气，可重也。」康有为《广艺舟双楫》称「小欧《道因碑》遒密峻整」。碑座龟趺两侧另有两组线刻胡人形象，卷发深目，馆方称为精绝。[西安碑林博物馆，道因法师碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

### English

Carved in the tenth month of 663, the third year of Longshuo, with text by Li Yan and calligraphy by Ouyang Tong. It has a chi-dragon head and a tortoise base, stands 312 cm high, and was moved to the Beilin in the Northern Song. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Stele of the Dharma Master Daoyin](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

The monk Daoyin (587–658) took refuge at the Duobao monastery outside Chengdu in the chaos at the end of the Sui; later he was summoned to the capital and stayed at the Da Ci’en monastery, where he “verified the Sanskrit texts with the Dharma Master Xuanzang”. The stele records his life. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Stele of the Dharma Master Daoyin](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

The calligrapher Ouyang Tong (625–691) was a son of Ouyang Xun. He first served as a Lang of the Lantai, was moved to the post of Secretariat Drafter in the Yifeng years and enfeoffed as Duke of Bohai; in the early Tianshou years he became director of the Ministry of Rites and concurrently handled the Chancellery, and in 691 he was made chancellor. For opposing the designation of Wu Chengsi as heir he was destroyed by the cruel official Lai Junchen. He was a master of regular script, “obtained his father’s method and surpassed it in steepness”; father and son were known together as the “greater and lesser Ouyangs”. Only two of his steles survive, the other being the Epitaph of Quan Nansheng. Wang Shizhen of the Ming wrote that the stele is “like the sick Vimalakirti, a man of high rank in poor clothes: not rich in joy, but with the air of frost and wind between the brows, worthy of respect.” Kang Youwei’s Guangyi zhou shuangji calls it “compact, dense and severe”. On the two sides of the tortoise base are two groups of line-carved foreigners with curly hair and deep-set eyes, which the museum calls superb. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Stele of the Dharma Master Daoyin](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

## 待考

### 中文

- 碑宽、厚与行款，所引馆方页未记，本记录不填。
- 碑石迁置的中间经过，馆方页未记。
- 欧阳通生年 625 依馆方页，未据墓志复核。

### English

- The cited museum page gives neither the stele’s width and thickness nor its line arrangement, so those are left open.
- The page does not record the intermediate moves of the stone.
- Ouyang Tong’s birth year of 625 follows the museum page and has not been checked against an epitaph.

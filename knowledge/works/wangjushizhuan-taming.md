# 王居士砖塔铭

## 背景

### 中文

唐《王居士砖塔铭》，显庆三年（658）刻于砖上，有方界格，楷书十七行、行十七字，上官灵芝撰、敬客书，敬客是唐高宗时人。上海图书馆展览说明记：明万历年间出土于西安城南百塔寺，出土时已裂为三块，继裂为五、再裂为七，且失去第一块，故全拓完本甚罕见；该馆藏初拓本经陆恭、缪曰芑、彭翰孙、潘承厚、吴湖帆等收藏，列国家一级文物。

### English

The Tang Brick Stupa Inscription for the Layman Wang was carved on brick in 658 with square ruled lines, in regular script of seventeen lines of seventeen characters; the text is by Shangguan Lingzhi and the writing by Jing Ke, a man of Emperor Gaozong's time. The Shanghai Library's exhibition note records that it was unearthed in the Wanli era at the Baita Temple south of Xi'an already split into three pieces, then five, then seven, with the first piece lost, so complete early rubbings are rare; the library's early impression passed through Lu Gong, Miao Yueqi, Peng Hansun, Pan Chenghou, Wu Hufan and others, and is listed as a first-grade cultural relic.

## 待考

### 中文

- 出土地「明万历、百塔寺」取上图口径；「明末清初、终南山楩梓谷」的说法未获官方页面支持，不作记录。
- 原石「虽存而残损过甚」与「石佚」两说并存，未作裁定。
- 石片分合与现存残石的下落未考。

### English

- The findspot follows the library's "Wanli era, Baita Temple"; the variant "late Ming–early Qing, Pianzi Valley, Zhongnan Mountains" lacks an official page and is not recorded.
- The stone's fate has two readings — surviving but badly broken, or lost — and neither is asserted.
- The pieces' breaking sequence and the present whereabouts of surviving fragments have not been examined.

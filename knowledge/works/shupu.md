# 书谱

## 背景

### 中文

《书谱》为孙过庭撰并书的草书论书长卷。台北故宫记创作时间为垂拱三年（687），现存释文开篇作「书谱卷上。吴郡孙过庭撰」，末尾署「垂拱三年（西元六八七年）写记」。正文自述「今撰为六篇，分成两卷」，并讨论真、草兼通，点画与使转，以及「五合」「五乖」等作书条件。本幅纸本，纵二十六点五厘米、横九百点八厘米。故宫博物院宋拓页称下卷已佚；台北故宫此页只记正文分六篇两卷，未就是否另有传世下卷墨迹作说明，两种记述并列。[国立故宫博物院，唐孙过庭书谱卷](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail?dep=P&id=14900) · [故宫博物院，宋拓孙过庭书谱](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234584.html)

隔水一有馆方归于宋徽宗的楷书题签「唐孙过庭书谱序下」，与卷首所写「书谱卷上」不同。故宫博物院《紫禁城》刊文记，上卷墨迹原藏清内府，后归故宫博物院，今在台湾；台北故宫另在本幅记梁清标、安岐等鉴藏印及《石渠宝笈初编》著录。[国立故宫博物院，书谱题签与印记](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail?dep=P&id=14900) · [故宫博物院《紫禁城》第113期，施安昌文](https://www.dpm.org.cn/impress/talk/208292.html)

### English

The Essay on Calligraphy is a long cursive treatise written by Sun Guoting in his own calligraphy. The National Palace Museum dates it to 687. The surviving transcription opens “upper scroll of the Essay on Calligraphy, composed by Sun Guoting of Wu Commandery” and closes “written down in the third year of Chuigong (687)”. Sun says that he composed six chapters in two juan and discusses the relationship of regular and cursive scripts, dots and strokes versus turning and transmission, and the “five harmonies” and “five discords” of writing. The paper painting section measures 26.5 by 900.8 cm. The Palace Museum’s Song-rubbing page says that the lower juan is lost; the National Palace Museum page records the six chapters in two juan but does not explain whether ink of a lower scroll survives elsewhere. Both accounts are kept. [National Palace Museum, Essay on Calligraphy](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail?dep=P&id=14900) · [Palace Museum, Song rubbing of Sun Guoting’s Shupu](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234584.html)

On the first mounting strip is a regular-script label attributed by the museum to Emperor Huizong, reading “lower preface of Sun Guoting’s Essay on Calligraphy”, which differs from the manuscript’s opening “upper scroll”. An article in the Palace Museum journal Zijincheng records that the upper scroll was held in the Qing imperial collection, later passed to the Palace Museum and is now in Taiwan. The National Palace Museum record also notes collector seals including Liang Qingbiao and An Qi, and the first Shiqu baoji catalogue. [National Palace Museum, label and seals](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail?dep=P&id=14900) · [Shi Anchang, Zijincheng no. 113](https://www.dpm.org.cn/impress/talk/208292.html)

## 待考

### 中文

- 正文自称六篇两卷，现存墨迹以「书谱卷上」起；故宫宋拓页称下卷已佚，台北故宫页未说明是否另有传世下卷墨迹，两种记述不合并。
- 宋徽宗题签作「序下」，与卷首「卷上」的关系未获馆方解释。
- 清宫至台北的具体交接年份及入藏日期，所引页面未记。

### English

- Sun describes six chapters in two juan, and the surviving manuscript opens as the “upper scroll”. The Palace Museum’s rubbing page calls the lower juan lost; the National Palace Museum page does not say whether ink of a lower scroll survives elsewhere. The two accounts are not merged.
- The Huizong label says “lower preface”, while the manuscript says “upper scroll”; the museum does not explain the difference.
- The cited pages give no exact year for the transfer from the Qing court to Taipei or for accession.

# 虞恭公碑

## 背景

### 中文

此为温彦博的墓碑，贞观十一年（637）十月立于醴泉昭陵：正书三十六行、行七十七字，篆额四行十六字。碑之下半入宋已断裂，故无全文拓本；传世最旧之本仅存八百余字，而全文约二千八百余字。

### English

A funerary stele for the chancellor Wen Yanbo, set up at Zhaoling in Liquan in the tenth month of 637: 36 lines of 77 characters, with a seal-script heading of four lines and sixteen characters. The lower half of the stone broke away in the Song, and no complete rubbing survives; the best early impressions carry only about eight hundred characters of roughly two thousand eight hundred.

## 待考

### 中文

- 「约二千八百余字」是国家图书馆叙录所记全文规模，不是任何传世拓本的实际存字。
- 本记录将原石与故宫藏南宋拓本分列为两件实物：拓本自有制作年代与收藏。
- 原石在昭陵，即唐太宗陵园，此碑为陪葬碑之一。本记录不描述陵园现状。

### English

- The character total is given as 约二千八百余字 by the National Library survey; it is not a count of any surviving impression.
- The record keeps the stone and the Palace Museum's Southern Song rubbing as separate objects, because a rubbing has its own production date and holding.
- The stone is at Zhaoling; that is the burial complex of Taizong, and the stele is one of its attendant monuments. The record does not describe the site's present arrangements.

## 导读

### 中文

这是温彦博的墓碑，唐贞观十一年（637）十月立于醴泉昭陵。岑文本撰文，欧阳询书；正书三十六行、行七十七字，碑额以篆书刻四行十六字。[故宫博物院《宋拓唐虞恭公温彦博碑》](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228414.html)

碑的下半在宋代已经断裂，所以没有全文拓本传世。传世最旧的拓本仅存八百余字；国家图书馆叙录记全文约二千八百余字，这是全文规模，不是任何一件拓本的实际存字。[国家图书馆《北京图书馆藏石刻叙录（十九）》](https://www.nlc.cn/upload/attachments/2026-02-14/40e09fcb.pdf)

本站把[原石](../catalogue/yugong-gong-bei.md#stele-yugong)与[故宫藏南宋拓本](../catalogue/yugong-gong-bei.md#rubbing-yugong-nansong)分列为两件实物。原石现存礼泉昭陵博物馆，下半剥泐；拓本则是剪裱册，馆方据“历”“高”二字未损定为南宋拓本。石与纸各有自己的年代与收藏，不能互相补足。[故宫博物院《温彦博碑》](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234129.html)

读这通碑，先看原石的存佚与下半剥泐，再看拓本存字；不要把“约二千八百余字”当成原石今日可读的字数，也不要把南宋拓本的存字当成全文。拓本上未见的内容，只能写“待访”或“待补”。[国家图书馆叙录](https://www.nlc.cn/upload/attachments/2026-02-14/40e09fcb.pdf)[故宫南宋拓本条目](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234129.html)

### English

This is the funerary stele of Wen Yanbo, set up at Zhaoling in Liquan in the tenth month of 637. Cen Wenben composed the text and Ouyang Xun wrote it; the inscription has thirty-six lines of seventy-seven characters, with a seal-script heading of four lines and sixteen characters. [Palace Museum, Song rubbing of the Wen Yanbo stele](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228414.html)

The lower half of the stone broke away in the Song, so no complete rubbing survives. The oldest surviving impression carries only about eight hundred characters; the National Library survey gives the full text as about two thousand eight hundred characters, a total for the composition, not a count for any surviving impression. [National Library of China, stone inscription survey 19](https://www.nlc.cn/upload/attachments/2026-02-14/40e09fcb.pdf)

This site records [the original stone](../catalogue/yugong-gong-bei.md#stele-yugong) and [the Palace Museum’s Southern Song rubbing](../catalogue/yugong-gong-bei.md#rubbing-yugong-nansong) as two objects. The stone is now in the Zhaoling Museum in Liquan, with its lower half effaced; the rubbing is a cut-and-mounted album, identified as Southern Song by the undamaged 历 and 高 characters. Stone and paper have their own dates and holdings and cannot fill each other’s gaps. [Palace Museum, Wen Yanbo stele](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234129.html)

When reading the stele, first establish what survives of the stone and how much of its lower half is effaced, then look at the characters preserved in the rubbing. Do not treat “about two thousand eight hundred characters” as the stone’s readable total today, or the rubbing’s surviving characters as the full text. What the rubbing does not show can only be marked as sought or supplied. [National Library survey](https://www.nlc.cn/upload/attachments/2026-02-14/40e09fcb.pdf) [Palace Museum, Southern Song rubbing](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234129.html)

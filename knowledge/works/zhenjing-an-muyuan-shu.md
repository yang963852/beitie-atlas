# 真镜庵募缘疏卷

## 背景

### 中文

杨维桢晚年为真镜庵所写的募缘启事。纸本，纵三十三点三厘米，横二百七十八点四厘米，卷装，行书。庵址原在浦东，今已不存。馆方称其「工书，用笔多章草意」，此卷正是标本：行草之中时时带入章草的收笔与波磔，墨色浓淡干湿对比极强，字距拉开、行距收紧，看去如乱石铺街。[上海博物馆，真镜庵募缘疏卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)

募缘疏本是寺院化缘的实用文字，写的人多半不会把它当作品。这一卷的分量恰恰在此：它先是启事，其次才是书法。读的时候可以先看它如何把「青铜钱多多选中」这类世俗句子写得理直气壮，再看笔法。[上海博物馆，真镜庵募缘疏卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)

### English

A fund-raising appeal Yang Weizhen wrote for the Zhenjing nunnery in his late years. Ink on paper, 33.3 by 278.4 cm, handscroll, running script. The nunnery stood in Pudong and no longer survives. The museum notes that his brushwork carries much of the clerical cursive manner, and this scroll is the specimen: draft-cursive strokes keep taking on the closed endings and flared tails of clerical script, the ink runs from wet to dry in violent contrast, and the characters are spaced wide while the columns are pulled tight, so the page looks like cobbles thrown down. [Shanghai Museum, Zhenjing nunnery notice](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)

A fund-raising notice is practical writing, and whoever writes one is not usually thinking of it as art. That is exactly what gives this scroll its weight: it is an appeal first and a piece of calligraphy second. One can begin by seeing how squarely it puts a worldly sentence — “the more bronze coins the better, just put them down under the heading” — and then look at the brush. [Shanghai Museum, Zhenjing nunnery notice](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)

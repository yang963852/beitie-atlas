# 鸭头丸帖

## 背景

### 中文

唐摹本。上海博物馆新藏品页把「工艺」标为行书，简介又称其代表王献之的草书成就；旧数字展页则记纸本，新藏品页记丝绢，书体表述与材质两说均并存，不静默合并。此卷经北宋宣和内府收藏，现存部分宣和裱。晚明为吴廷族兄吴希元所藏，董其昌与王肯堂因此得观并题跋。馆方以「外拓」用笔与连绵相属的「一笔书」说明王献之在父书之外的变革。

### English

A Tang tracing copy. The Shanghai Museum's newer collection record labels its craft as running script, while its description calls the scroll a representative work of Wang Xianzhi's cursive achievement; both formulations are kept. The older digital-exhibition page says paper, whereas the newer record says silk, so the material is left as a documented conflict rather than silently reconciled. The scroll passed through the Northern Song Xuanhe collection, and part of the Xuanhe mounting survives. In the late Ming it belonged to Wu Xiyuan, a cousin of Wu Ting, which gave Dong Qichang and Wang Kentang the chance to see it and add colophons. The museum explains Wang Xianzhi's departure from his father's manner through his outward-turning brush and the linked momentum later called one-stroke writing.

# 行穰帖

## 背景

### 中文

两馆均把此信归于王羲之。台北故宫将所藏一卷标为草书，上博展品页则未标书体。馆方著录普林斯顿与台北两件不同实物，本库分别建档，不推定二者的制作关系。

普林斯顿本为唐代双钩摹本，馆方认为应是原信的前半。文字最早著录于张彦远《法书要录》，后入北宋宣和内府；北宋亡后，金人王庭筠刻入《雪溪堂帖》。明代经项元汴、韩世能、董其昌鉴藏，董其昌以此帖换得李唐《江山小景图》卷，友人吴廷收得后刻入《余清斋法帖》。今藏普林斯顿大学艺术博物馆，John B. Elliott 遗赠。这些递藏与刻帖信息只属于普林斯顿本。

台北故宫本为纸本，本幅纵31.7、横13厘米，文物统一编号故書000377N000000000。馆方题跋栏录有董其昌万历甲辰（1604）冬十月二十三日跋，其中述及宣和、政和印并推为内府旧藏；这是董其昌的鉴藏意见，不等于馆方已鉴定为王羲之亲笔。馆方未记此本的制作年代与方式。

### English

Both museums attribute the letter to Wang Xizhi. The National Palace Museum labels its scroll as cursive, while the Shanghai Museum exhibition page gives no script label. The two museums describe different physical objects, catalogued separately here without inferring how they are related.

The Princeton scroll is a Tang double-outline tracing copy, which that museum takes to be the first half of the letter. The text is first recorded in Zhang Yanyuan's ninth-century Fashu yaolu and entered the Northern Song Xuanhe collection; after the dynasty fell, Wang Tingyun engraved it in the Xuexitang modelbooks. In the Ming it passed through Xiang Yuanbian, Han Shineng and Dong Qichang, who traded it for Li Tang's River Scenes; his friend Wu Ting then had it engraved in the Yuqingzhai modelbooks. It is now in the Princeton University Art Museum, the John B. Elliott bequest. This transmission and engraving history belongs to the Princeton copy alone.

The National Palace Museum scroll is on paper, with a main sheet 31.7 by 13 cm, under object number 故書000377N000000000. Its colophon tab records Dong Qichang's inscription of the twenty-third day of the tenth lunar month in Wanli Jiachen (1604), in which he discusses Xuanhe and Zhenghe seals and treats the scroll as a former palace holding. That is Dong's judgment, not a museum authentication of an autograph by Wang Xizhi. The museum gives neither a production date nor a production method for this scroll.

## 待考

### 中文

- 作品书体采用台北故宫的「草书」分类；普林斯顿所据上博页面未标书体。
- 「应是信札前半」只是上博对普林斯顿本的推测，本记录不作定论。
- 普林斯顿本的藏品号与装裱、题跋现状未见于此页。
- 台北故宫本未记制作年代和制作方式；董其昌的鉴藏意见不等于馆方鉴定。
- 两件实物的制作关系未见证据，不推定其为摹本、拓本或同一祖本。

### English

- The work-level script follows the National Palace Museum's cursive classification; the Shanghai Museum page used for the Princeton copy gives no script label.
- "Should be the first half" is the Shanghai Museum's inference about the Princeton copy, not a settled conclusion.
- Neither an accession number nor the present mounting and colophons of the Princeton copy appear on that page.
- The National Palace Museum gives no production date or method for its scroll; Dong Qichang's opinion is not a museum authentication.
- No evidence establishes the production relationship between the two objects, so none is inferred.

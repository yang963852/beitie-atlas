# 王宠

## 中文

王宠（1494—1533），字履仁，后改履吉，号雅宜山人，吴县（今苏州）人。为邑诸生，自十七岁至三十八岁八次应乡试，屡试不取，最后以诸生贡入太学，人称王贡士。工诗文，精书法，能山水，工篆刻；与祝允明、文徵明并称吴中三家，又是唐寅的姻亲——其子娶唐氏女。[故宫博物院，送陈子龄会试诗页](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229599.html)

他的小楷取法晋唐，学智永、虞世南、褚遂良，清雅简淡，萧散含蓄，拙趣有姿。馆方用「婉丽遒逸」四字概括他的书风；看起来松，其实是把紧藏在里面——结字宽绰，行距拉开，一笔一画都不肯放快。[故宫博物院，送陈子龄会试诗页](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229599.html)

库内收两件。《[游包山集](../works/you-baoshan-ji.md)》是他三十四岁那年，把八年前游包山所作的诗用小楷抄给友人补庵居士，纸本，纵二十一点六、横三百二十三点五厘米，文嘉见后称「天才妙绝」，历来推为小楷代表作；刘九庵则疑此作未必出自王宠之手，两说并存。[上海博物馆，历代书法馆换展](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004231)《[送陈子龄会试诗页](../works/song-chen-ziling.md)》为门人赴会试所作三首，楷书十八行，纸本，纵二十三点二、横三十六点三厘米，馆方按风格定为晚年之笔。[故宫博物院，送陈子龄会试诗页](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229599.html)

## English

Wang Chong (1494–1533), courtesy name Lüren and later Lüji, styled Yayi shanren, was a native of Wu county, present-day Suzhou. A county student, he sat the provincial examination eight times between the ages of seventeen and thirty-eight and never passed, entering the Imperial College at last as a tribute student, from which he was called Scholar Wang. He wrote poetry, excelled at calligraphy, painted landscapes and practised seal carving; with Zhu Yunming and Wen Zhengming he is counted among the three masters of Wu, and he was kin to Tang Yin by marriage, his son taking a Tang daughter. [Palace Museum, poem leaf for Chen Ziling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229599.html)

His small regular script takes its models from the Jin and Tang, studying Zhiyong, Yu Shinan and Chu Suiliang; it is clear and plain, loose and reserved, awkward in a way that holds charm. The museum sums his manner as “graceful and vigorous”: it looks relaxed, but the tightness is kept inside — broad frames, wide spacing, and not one stroke allowed to hurry. [Palace Museum, poem leaf for Chen Ziling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229599.html)

Two works are held here. The [Baoshan excursion album](../works/you-baoshan-ji.md) was written at thirty-four, when he copied out poems from a trip eight years earlier for his friend the layman Bu’an: ink on paper, 21.6 by 323.5 cm. Wen Jia called it “genius beyond compare”, and it has long been taken as his masterpiece in small script; Liu Jiuan doubted that the hand was Wang’s at all, and both views stand. [Shanghai Museum, rotation of the calligraphy gallery](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004231) The [poem leaf for Chen Ziling](../works/song-chen-ziling.md), three poems written for a disciple going up to the metropolitan examination, runs to eighteen lines of regular script, 23.2 by 36.3 cm, and is dated by style to his late years. [Palace Museum, poem leaf for Chen Ziling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229599.html)

# 欧阳通

## 中文

欧阳通（625—691），[欧阳询](../people/ouyang-xun.md)之子。初拜兰台郎，仪凤中迁中书舍人，封渤海公；天授初年转司礼卿判纳言事，二年（691）为相，因反立武承嗣为太子，为酷吏来俊臣陷害而死。[西安碑林博物馆，道因法师碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

龙朔三年（663）十月，他书[《道因法师碑》](../works/daoyin-fashi-bei.md)，李俨撰文。碑为螭首龟趺，通高三百一十二厘米，北宋移存西安碑林。碑主道因法师（587—658）隋末避乱益州多宝寺，后赴京止大慈恩寺，与玄奘证译梵本；碑记其生平。[西安碑林博物馆，道因法师碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

他工楷书，得父法而险峻过之，父子齐名，号「大小欧阳」。所留碑版仅有二种，另一种为《泉男生墓志》。明王世贞评此碑：「如病维摩，高格贫士，虽不饶乐，而眉宇间有风霜之气，可重也。」康有为《广艺舟双楫》称「小欧《道因碑》遒密峻整」。碑座龟趺两侧另有两组线刻胡人形象，卷发深目，馆方称为精绝。[西安碑林博物馆，道因法师碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

### English

Ouyang Tong (625–691) was a son of [Ouyang Xun](../people/ouyang-xun.md). He first served as a Lang of the Lantai, was moved to the post of Secretariat Drafter in the Yifeng years and enfeoffed as Duke of Bohai; in the early Tianshou years he became director of the Ministry of Rites and concurrently handled the Chancellery, and in 691 he was made chancellor. For opposing the designation of Wu Chengsi as heir he was destroyed by the cruel official Lai Junchen. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Stele of the Dharma Master Daoyin](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

In the tenth month of 663 he wrote the [Stele of the Dharma Master Daoyin](../works/daoyin-fashi-bei.md), with text by Li Yan. The stele has a chi-dragon head and a tortoise base, stands 312 cm high, and was moved to the Beilin in the Northern Song. Its subject, Daoyin (587–658), took refuge at the Duobao monastery in Chengdu at the end of the Sui, later went to the capital and stayed at the Da Ci’en monastery, where he verified Sanskrit texts with Xuanzang; the stele records his life. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Stele of the Dharma Master Daoyin](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

He was a master of regular script, “obtained his father’s method and surpassed it in steepness”; father and son were known together as the “greater and lesser Ouyangs”. Only two of his steles survive, the other being the Epitaph of Quan Nansheng. Wang Shizhen of the Ming wrote that the stele is “like the sick Vimalakirti, a man of high rank in poor clothes: not rich in joy, but with the air of frost and wind between the brows, worthy of respect.” Kang Youwei’s Guangyi zhou shuangji calls it “compact, dense and severe”. On the two sides of the tortoise base are two groups of line-carved foreigners with curly hair and deep-set eyes, which the museum calls superb. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Stele of the Dharma Master Daoyin](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2598)

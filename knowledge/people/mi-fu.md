# 米芾

## 中文

米芾（1051—1107），字元章，号鹿门居士、襄阳漫士、海岳外史，祖籍太原，后迁襄阳，晚居润州（今江苏镇江）。徽宗时官书学博士，擢礼部员外郎，人称“米南宫”。故宫博物院的小传称其书法“初宗二王、颜真卿，后博采众长，自成一家”，用笔“八面出锋”，结体欹侧多姿，与蔡襄、苏轼、黄庭坚并称“北宋四大家”。[故宫博物院，米芾行书苕溪诗卷·米芾](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228246.html)

读米芾，可从元祐三年（1088）一年里的两卷入手。[《苕溪诗卷》](../works/tiaoxi.md)为纸本行书，三十五行二百九十四字，末署“元祐戊辰八月八日作”，馆方据此定在 1088 年，时米芾三十八岁；卷首自题“將之苕溪戲作呈諸友，襄陽漫仕黻”，所书是他自撰的六首诗。清人吴其贞《书画记》评此卷“运笔潇洒，结构舒畅，盖效颜鲁公化书者”——馆方引此语，说它道出了米芾宗法[颜真卿](../people/yan-zhenqing.md)又自出新意的路子。同年所书的[《蜀素帖》](../works/shusu.md)，可与此卷对看。[故宫博物院，米芾行书苕溪诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228246.html) · [国立故宫博物院，蜀素帖](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/912?dep=P)

一卷怎样被认作真迹，此卷末尾留着材料：其子米友仁跋“右呈諸友等詩，先臣芾真迹，臣米友仁鑑定恭跋”。由儿子出面，称“先臣”，作“鉴定恭跋”，这一行字本身就是递藏链的第一环。此卷后入南宋绍兴内府，经项元汴、梁清标诸家，终归乾隆内府，并刻入[《三希堂法帖》](../works/sanxitang-fatie.md)。[故宫博物院，米芾行书苕溪诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228246.html)

米芾另有一面留在[《珊瑚帖》](../works/shanhu.md)里。那是他与人谈收藏的一封书信：收了张僧繇的天王、景温的《问礼图》，又提到一枝珊瑚笔架；说到珊瑚，索性随手把笔架画在信里。馆方称此为“米芾唯一的传世画迹”。他本以水墨山水立“米家山”之名，这里留下的却是一个笔架的小图——看米芾的画，不妨从这个“随手”入手。[故宫博物院，米芾行书珊瑚帖页](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/231255.html)

## English

Mi Fu (1051–1107), courtesy name Yuanzhang, called himself Lumen Jushi, Xiangyang Manshi and Haiyue Waishi. His ancestral home was Taiyuan; the family moved to Xiangyang and he settled late in life at Runzhou, present-day Zhenjiang in Jiangsu. Under Huizong he served as Erudite of Calligraphy and was promoted to Vice Director of the Ministry of Rites, which earned him the name Mi Nangong. The Palace Museum’s short biography says his calligraphy “first took the Two Wangs and Yan Zhenqing as its model, then gathered from many masters to form a manner of its own,” with a brush that “issues from eight facets,” and that his characters lean and vary; it counts him with Cai Xiang, Su Shi and Huang Tingjian as the Four Masters of the Northern Song. [Palace Museum, Mi Fu’s Tiaoxi poems, biography of Mi Fu](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228246.html)

A way into Mi Fu is the two scrolls he wrote in one year, the third of Yuanyou (1088). The [Tiaoxi Poems](../works/tiaoxi.md) is ink on paper, running script, thirty-five lines and 294 characters, ending with the date “the eighth day of the eighth month, wuchen year of Yuanyou”; the museum dates it to 1088 on that basis, when Mi Fu was thirty-eight. The opening line, “playing at this as I go to Tiaoxi, presented to my friends, the Xiangyang idle official Fu,” shows the poems are his own, six of them. The Qing connoisseur Wu Qizhen judged in his Shuhua Ji that the scroll “moves the brush freely and its structure is relaxed, for he is following Yan Lugong’s transformed manner”; the museum quotes this to show how Mi Fu took [Yan Zhenqing](../people/yan-zhenqing.md) as his model and then made something new. The [Poems on Shu Silk](../works/shusu.md), written the same year, can be read against it. [Palace Museum, Mi Fu’s Tiaoxi poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228246.html) · [National Palace Museum, Poems on Shu Silk](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/912?dep=P)

How a scroll comes to be accepted as genuine is documented at the end of this one: Mi Fu’s son Mi Youren wrote a colophon — “the poems presented to my friends above are the true trace of my late father Fu, respectfully appraised and colophoned by his subject Mi Youren.” A son speaking, calling himself a subject, appraising in a colophon: that line is itself the first link in the chain of transmission. The scroll later entered the Shaoxing palace collection of the Southern Song, passed through Xiang Yuanbian and Liang Qingbiao, and ended in the Qianlong emperor’s collection, where it was cut into the [Sanxitang Model Letters](../works/sanxitang-fatie.md). [Palace Museum, Mi Fu’s Tiaoxi poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228246.html)

Another side of Mi Fu survives in the [Coral Letter](../works/shanhu.md), a note to a friend about collecting: he has acquired Zhang Sengyou’s Heavenly King and Xie Jingwen’s Painting of the Question of Rites, and mentions a coral brush-rest. Coming to the coral, he simply drew the rest into the letter. The museum calls this “the only surviving painting by Mi Fu.” He made his name in landscape with the “Mi family mountains”; what remains here is a small drawing of a brush-rest — a good place to begin with Mi Fu as a painter. [Palace Museum, Mi Fu’s Coral Letter](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/231255.html)

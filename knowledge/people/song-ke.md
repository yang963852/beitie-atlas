# 宋克

## 中文

故宫博物院记宋克（1327—1387），字仲温，长洲（今苏州市）人，家居南宫里。小传称其少任侠，壮年学兵法；张士诚据吴时，虽被罗致而不就，后杜门谢客，专意翰墨。洪武初征为侍书，出为凤翔府同知。[故宫博物院，宋克章草书急就章卷·宋克](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230117.html)

馆方称其擅长真、草书，师法钟繇、王羲之，尤工章草，沿袭[赵孟頫](zhao-mengfu.md)、邓文原余绪，风格古雅劲健而自成面目；与宋璲、宋广并称“三宋”。本库所收[《急就章》](../works/song-jijiuzhang.md)为宋克1370年书写、故宫藏新00098058号纸本卷。

## English

The Palace Museum dates Song Ke to 1327–1387, gives his courtesy name as Zhongwen, and identifies him as a native of Changzhou, present-day Suzhou, whose home was in Nangongli. Its biography describes his youthful martial pursuits and later study of military strategy. When Zhang Shicheng held Wu, Song declined Zhang’s recruitment; he subsequently withdrew from visitors and devoted himself to writing. Early in the Hongwu reign he was summoned as an imperial calligrapher and later served as vice prefect of Fengxiang. [Palace Museum, Song Ke’s zhangcao scroll of the Jijiu Chapters, “Song Ke” entry](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230117.html)

The museum says that he excelled in regular and cursive scripts, studied Zhong You and Wang Xizhi, and was especially accomplished in zhangcao. It places his ancient, vigorous and individually formed manner after [Zhao Mengfu](zhao-mengfu.md) and Deng Wenyuan, and records that he was grouped with Song Yi and Song Guang as the “Three Songs.” This library’s [*Jijiu Chapters*](../works/song-jijiuzhang.md) is the paper scroll he wrote in 1370, held by the Palace Museum under accession number 新00098058.

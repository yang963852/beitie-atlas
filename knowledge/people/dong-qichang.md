# 董其昌

## 中文

### 生平与身份

董其昌（1555—1636），字玄宰，号思白、香光居士，华亭（今上海松江）人。故宫记明万历十六年（1588）进士、官至礼部尚书、卒谥文敏；上海博物馆记万历十七年（1589）进士、官至南京礼部尚书、卒谥文敏。两说并存，本库不取其一。[故宫博物院，董其昌行书论书卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230546.html) · [上海博物馆，丹青宝筏——董其昌书画艺术大展](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004172)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于明。

### 书体与师承

书体与师承待考——本库现有来源未逐字写明其师承。故宫记其精书画鉴赏、富收藏，在书画理论上主张「南北宗」，工书法，自谓于率易中得之，董派书风对当时及清初书坛影响极大；上博亦记「以禅喻画」倡导南北宗论，并指出其赝品泛滥、世称「烂董」，为鉴定难点。[故宫博物院，董其昌行书论书卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230546.html) · [上海博物馆，董其昌书画艺术大展](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004172)

### 代表作与本库关联

故宫著录《行书论书》卷，纸本行书，纵二十八厘米、横一百九十厘米，钤「董其昌印」；据自跋书于万历十九年（1591），为中年之作，卷后另有万历三十九年（1611）复见此卷所写跋。上博记其大展遴选董其昌及相关作品一百五十四件（组），设「作品辨伪」专节。[故宫博物院，董其昌行书论书卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230546.html) · [上海博物馆，董其昌书画艺术大展](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004172)

## English

### Life and identity

Dong Qichang (1555–1636), courtesy name Xuanzai, style names Sibai and Xiangguang Jushi, was a native of Huating, present-day Songjiang in Shanghai. The Palace Museum records that he passed the examination in 1588, rose to minister of rites and received the posthumous name Wenmin; the Shanghai Museum gives 1589 and Nanjing minister of rites, with the same posthumous name. Both readings are kept and neither is preferred. [Palace Museum, Dong Qichang's essay on calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230546.html) · [Shanghai Museum, exhibition of Dong Qichang's painting and calligraphy](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004172). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Ming.

### Scripts and models

Scripts and teacher lineage remain open; the cited sources do not transcribe them. The Palace Museum records his connoisseurship and collection, his "northern and southern schools" theory, his calligraphy "arrived at through ease," and the great influence of his style; the Shanghai Museum likewise notes his advocacy of the theory and that forgeries were so widespread they were called "rotten Dong," a byword in authentication. [Palace Museum, essay on calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230546.html) · [Shanghai Museum, exhibition](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004172)

### Works in this library

The museum records the essay on calligraphy in running script, ink on paper, 28 by 190 cm, bearing Dong's seal; from his own colophon it was written in 1591, a middle-period work, with a second colophon of 1611 on seeing it again. The Shanghai Museum's exhibition gathered 154 works and related pieces and included a section on authentication. [Palace Museum, essay on calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/230546.html) · [Shanghai Museum, exhibition](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/I00004172)

# 祝允明

## 中文

### 生平与身份

祝允明（1460—1526），字希哲，号枝山，长洲（今江苏苏州）人，官广东兴宁县知县，后转应天府通判，世称「祝京兆」。[上海博物馆，草书贾至早朝诗轴](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00166290)。上海博物馆另一页作 1460—1527，并记其因右手六指自号「枝指生」，与唐寅、文徵明、徐祯卿齐名，为「吴中四才子」之一。卒年两说并存，本库不取其一。[上海博物馆，草书牡丹赋卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00000459)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于明。

### 书体与师承

上博记其工书、各体皆精、尤长狂草，为吴门书派代表；另一页记其得法于张旭、怀素，并受黄庭坚影响而更狂放烂漫，中年多行草、晚年喜大草。此为馆方所述书风与渊源。[上海博物馆，草书贾至早朝诗轴](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00166290) · [上海博物馆，草书牡丹赋卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00000459)

上海博物馆著录多件：草书《秋日闲居诗》扇页（金笺，纵十八点八、横五十二厘米）、草书《贾至早朝诗》轴（纸本，吴门派，杨瓞緜等捐赠）、草书《牡丹赋》卷（纸本，纵三十点八、横九百二十五点五厘米，戚叔玉 1980 年捐赠）。本库未另建这些作品档案，此处只记馆方所述。[上海博物馆，草书贾至早朝诗轴](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00166290) · [上海博物馆，草书牡丹赋卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00000459)

## English

### Life and identity

Zhu Yunming (1460–1526), courtesy name Xizhe, style name Zhishan, was a native of Changzhou, present-day Suzhou in Jiangsu. He served as magistrate of Xingning in Guangdong and later as vice prefect of the capital, from which he was known as Zhu Jingzhao. [Shanghai Museum, cursive scroll of Jia Zhi's poem at dawn](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00166290). Another Shanghai Museum page gives 1460–1527 and records that, having six fingers on his right hand, he called himself Zhizhisheng, and that he was one of the Four Talents of Wu with Tang Yin, Wen Zhengming and Xu Zhenqing. Both death years are kept and neither is preferred. [Shanghai Museum, cursive scroll of the Peony Fu](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00000459). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Ming.

### Scripts and models

The museum records that he excelled at writing in every script, especially wild cursive, and was a representative of the Wu school; another page says he took his method from Zhang Xu and Huaisu and was influenced by Huang Tingjian, becoming yet more uninhibited, with running cursive in middle age and large cursive in later years. [Shanghai Museum, Jia Zhi poem](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00166290) · [Shanghai Museum, Peony Fu](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00000459)

### Works in this library

The Shanghai Museum records several works: a cursive fan leaf of Poems on Leisure in Autumn (gold paper, 18.8 by 52 cm), a cursive scroll of Jia Zhi's poem at dawn (paper, Wu school, gift of Yang Diemian and others), and a cursive scroll of the Peony Fu (paper, 30.8 by 925.5 cm, gift of Qi Shuyu in 1980). This library holds no separate work records; only the museum's accounts are given. [Shanghai Museum, Jia Zhi poem](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00166290) · [Shanghai Museum, Peony Fu](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00000459)

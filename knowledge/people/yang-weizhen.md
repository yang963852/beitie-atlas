# 杨维桢

## 中文

杨维桢（1296—1370），字廉夫，号铁崖，又号铁笛道人、抱遗叟，浙江山阴人。青年苦读，泰定四年（1327）中进士，署天台尹，后官建德路总管府推官；元末擢江西等处儒学提举，未及上任而兵乱起，遂避居富春山，徙钱塘，晚年寓松江。明洪武二年（1369）召至南京纂修礼乐书，不久辞归，抵家而卒。诗风奇诡，号「铁崖体」。[故宫博物院，张栻城南杂咏卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html)（上海博物馆作 1297—1370）

他的字在元代是一片异色。当整个书坛都在赵孟頫的圆润里讨生活，他把章草、汉隶、今草搅在一处，结字大小参差，行距大于字距，拗强苍劲，别自成家。吴宽形容为「大将班师，三军奏凯，破斧缺斨，倒载而归」，徐有贞说「铁崖狂怪不经，而步履自高」——两句都不是贬语。[故宫博物院，张栻城南杂咏卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html)

库内收他两件。《[真镜庵募缘疏卷](../works/zhenjing-an-muyuan-shu.md)》是他晚年为浦东真镜庵写的募缘启事，纸本，纵三十三点三、横二百七十八点四厘米，用笔多章草意，浓淡干湿对比极强。[上海博物馆，真镜庵募缘疏卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)《[城南唱和诗卷](../works/chengnan-changhe-shi.md)》书于至正二十二年（1362），时年六十七，是替虞子贤补书张栻《城南杂咏》二十首，纸本，纵三十一点六、横二百一十六点六厘米。[故宫博物院，张栻城南杂咏卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html)

### 生平与身份

上文所记生卒、字号、籍贯与历官沿用故宫条目；上海博物馆所附小传逐字作「1297—1370，字廉夫，号铁崖，浙江诸暨人，泰定四年进士，官杭州四务提举，元末避乱徙松江」，与上文 1296—1370、浙江山阴人、署天台尹、后官建德路总管府推官有出入。生卒、籍贯、官职两说并存，不取其一。[上海博物馆，真镜庵募缘疏卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于元。

### 书体与师承

上文已记其把章草、汉隶、今草搅在一处、别自成家；上海博物馆小传称其「工书，用笔多章草意」，两说相合。[上海博物馆，真镜庵募缘疏卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收[《真镜庵募缘疏卷》](../works/zhenjing-an-muyuan-shu.md)为晚年为浦东真镜庵所写募缘启事，纸本，用笔多章草意；[《城南唱和诗卷》](../works/chengnan-changhe-shi.md)书于至正二十二年（1362），时年六十七。故宫著录后一件书于 1362 年、并录吴宽与徐有贞评语。[故宫博物院，张栻城南杂咏卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html)


### 可临之作

- [《真镜庵募缘疏卷》](../works/zhenjing-an-muyuan-shu.md)：杨维桢晚年为真镜庵写的行书募缘启事，馆方称其用笔多章草意。
- [《城南唱和诗卷》](../works/chengnan-changhe-shi.md)：杨维桢1362年追和张栻《城南杂咏》二十首的行书纸本。

## English

Yang Weizhen (1296–1370), courtesy name Lianfu, styled Tieya and later Tiedi daoren and Baoyi sou, was a native of Shanyin in Zhejiang. He studied hard in youth, passed the metropolitan examination in 1327, served as magistrate of Tiantai and then as judge of the Jiande circuit; at the end of the Yuan he was promoted to the Jiangxi educational commission but the disorders broke out before he could take up the post, so he retired to Mount Fuchun, moved to Qiantang and spent his last years in Songjiang. In 1369 the Ming court summoned him to Nanjing to compile the ritual and music books; he soon asked leave and died after reaching home. His poetry, strange and forceful, was known as the Iron Cliff manner. [Palace Museum, Zhang Shi’s South of the City poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html) (Shanghai Museum gives 1297–1370)

His writing is a discord in Yuan calligraphy. While the whole field lived off Zhao Mengfu’s roundness, he mixed clerical, draft-cursive and modern cursive together, let characters vary wildly in size, opened the line spacing wider than the column spacing, and made a manner of his own out of awkward strength. Wu Kuan described it as a great general returning with his army, drums beating, axes chipped, halberds carried backwards; Xu Youzhen called it “wild and unorthodox, yet every step high”. Neither was an insult. [Palace Museum, Zhang Shi’s South of the City poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html)

The library holds two works. The [fund-raising notice for the Zhenjing nunnery](../works/zhenjing-an-muyuan-shu.md) is a late-life appeal written for a nunnery in Pudong: ink on paper, 33.3 by 278.4 cm, heavy with clerical-cursive strokes and violent contrasts of wet and dry ink. [Shanghai Museum, Zhenjing nunnery notice](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621) The [South of the City poems](../works/chengnan-changhe-shi.md) was written in 1362, when he was sixty-seven, supplying Zhang Shi’s twenty poems for Yu Zixian: ink on paper, 31.6 by 216.6 cm. [Palace Museum, Zhang Shi’s South of the City poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html)

### Life and identity

The dates, courtesy name, native place and offices above are kept from the Palace Museum entry. The Shanghai Museum's attached biography reads verbatim "1297–1370, courtesy name Lianfu, styled Tieya, a native of Zhuji in Zhejiang, metropolitan graduate of 1327, director of the Hangzhou four offices, who moved to Songjiang in the late-Yuan disorders," which differs from the 1296–1370, Shanyin native, Tiantai magistrate and Jiande judge given above. The dates, native place and offices are kept in both readings, neither preferred. [Shanghai Museum, Zhenjing nunnery notice](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Yuan.

### Scripts and models

The account above of mixing clerical, draft-cursive and modern cursive into a manner of his own is kept; the Shanghai Museum biography likewise calls him "skilled at writing, his brush much imbued with clerical-cursive intent," which agrees. [Shanghai Museum, Zhenjing nunnery notice](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000621)

### Works in this library

The [fund-raising notice for the Zhenjing nunnery](../works/zhenjing-an-muyuan-shu.md) is a late-life appeal for a Pudong nunnery, ink on paper, much imbued with clerical-cursive intent; the [South of the City poems](../works/chengnan-changhe-shi.md) was written in 1362 at sixty-seven. The Palace Museum dates the latter to 1362 and records the judgements of Wu Kuan and Xu Youzhen. [Palace Museum, Zhang Shi's South of the City poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/vr/stone-moat/wyd_31.html)

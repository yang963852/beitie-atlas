# 虞世南

## 中文

虞世南（558—638），字伯施，越州余姚人。官至秘书少监，封永兴县公，世称虞永兴。师事智永，得二王法；与欧阳询、褚遂良、薛稷并称初唐四家。[上海博物馆，汝南公主墓志铭卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641)

初唐四家里，欧取险、褚取变，虞世南守的是一条中庸的路。他的楷书不外露筋骨，转折处圆而含力，读久了才见其紧；行书也一样，看似萧散，笔笔都扣得住。这一路数上接智永，下开褚遂良的早年面貌。

库内两件可以并看。一是[《孔子庙堂碑》](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md)：原石贞观间已毁，本库所收是北宋建隆至乾德间的西庙堂重刻，落款里另记了重立者王彦超与刻工安祚，没把宋刻当唐石。[西安碑林博物馆，孔子庙堂碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2596) 二是[《汝南公主墓志铭》](../works/runan-gongzhu-muzhi.md)：贞观十年（636）为太宗第三女所写的墓志草稿，纸本，纵二十五点九、横三十八点四厘米，是他唯一接近真迹的墨迹。因笔意圆活，旧有「米临」一说，馆方两说并存。[上海博物馆，汝南公主墓志铭卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641)

### 生平与身份

上文所记生卒、字号、籍贯、官爵与师承沿用上海博物馆所附小传原文：558—638，字伯施，浙江余姚人，官秘书少监，封永兴县公，师智永，初唐四家之一；并记其书于贞观十年（636）时年七十九。[上海博物馆，汝南公主墓志铭卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于唐。

### 书体与师承

上文已记师事智永、得二王法。上海博物馆小传亦列「师智永」与「初唐四家」；书于 636 年墓志的笔意圆活，旧有「米临」一说，馆方两说并存。[上海博物馆，汝南公主墓志铭卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收[《孔子庙堂碑》](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md)与[《汝南公主墓志铭》](../works/runan-gongzhu-muzhi.md)。西安碑林记西庙堂重刻的尺寸、行数与刻工；陕西省文物志另记原石 629—630、重刻 961—964 与不同尺寸，两说并存，本库不改写成一种。[西安碑林博物馆，孔子庙堂碑](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2596)


### 可临之作

- [《孔子庙堂碑》](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md)：虞世南629至630年间书的楷书碑，原石早毁，今见北宋重刻的西庙堂本。
- [《汝南公主墓志铭》](../works/runan-gongzhu-muzhi.md)：虞世南636年为汝南公主撰墓志的行书纸本草稿，因是草稿无款且后半残缺。

## English

Yu Shinan (558–638), courtesy name Boshi, was a native of Yuyao in Yue prefecture. He rose to vice director of the imperial library and was enfeoffed as duke of Yongxing county, from which he was known as Yu Yongxing. He studied under the monk Zhiyong and inherited the manner of the Two Wangs; with Ouyang Xun, Chu Suiliang and Xue Ji he is counted among the Four Masters of the Early Tang. [Shanghai Museum, Epitaph for Princess Runan](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641)

Among the four, Ouyang took the steep and Chu the changing path; Yu held to the middle. His regular script does not show its sinew on the surface, and its turns are round with the strength kept inside; only long looking reveals how tightly it is built. His running script is the same — apparently loose, yet every stroke holds. The line runs back to Zhiyong and forward into Chu Suiliang’s early manner.

Two works in this library can be read together. The first is the [Stele of the Confucius Temple](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md): the original stone was destroyed in the Zhenguan period, and what the library holds is the Northern Song recutting known as the Western Temple version, with the re-erector Wang Yanchao and the cutter An Zuo recorded separately so that the Song stone is not taken for the Tang one. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Confucius Temple Stele](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2596) The second is the [Epitaph for Princess Runan](../works/runan-gongzhu-muzhi.md), a draft written in 636 for Taizong’s third daughter: ink on paper, 25.9 by 38.4 cm, and the only manuscript of his that comes close to an autograph. Its rounded, lively brushwork has long prompted a minority view that it is a copy by Mi Fu, and the museum keeps both attributions open. [Shanghai Museum, Epitaph for Princess Runan](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641)

### Life and identity

The dates, courtesy name, native place, offices and teacher given above are kept from the museum's attached biography: 558–638, courtesy name Boshi, a native of Yuyao in Zhejiang, vice director of the imperial library, duke of Yongxing county, a student of Zhiyong and one of the Four Masters of the Early Tang; the epitaph was written in 636 when he was seventy-nine. [Shanghai Museum, Epitaph for Princess Runan](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Tang.

### Scripts and models

The study under Zhiyong and the Two Wangs manner are noted above; the museum biography likewise lists Zhiyong and the Four Masters. The rounded brushwork of the 636 epitaph has prompted the minority view that it is a Mi Fu copy, and the museum keeps both attributions open. [Shanghai Museum, Epitaph for Princess Runan](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00004641)

### Works in this library

The library holds the [Stele of the Confucius Temple](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md) and the [Epitaph for Princess Runan](../works/runan-gongzhu-muzhi.md). The Beilin Museum records the size, line count and cutter of the Western Temple recutting; a provincial heritage compilation gives the original as 629–630, the recut as 961–964, with different measurements. Both readings are kept and not reduced to one. [Xi'an Beilin Museum, Confucius Temple Stele](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2596)

### Works to copy

- [Stele of the Confucius Temple](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md): A regular-script stele written by Yu Shinan around 629–630, whose original was soon destroyed and survives in the Northern Song recutting of the Western Temple version.
- [Epitaph for Princess Runan](../works/runan-gongzhu-muzhi.md): Yu Shinan’s 636 running-script paper draft of Princess Runan’s epitaph, unsigned because it was a draft and incomplete at the end.

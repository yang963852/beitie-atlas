# 杨凝式

## 中文

杨凝式生于唐末，在后梁、唐、晋、汉、周五代朝廷都做过官，官至太子太保；曾佯疯自晦，因此有“杨风子”的雅号。黄庭坚有一首绝句专说他：“世人尽学兰亭面，欲换凡骨无金丹。谁知洛阳杨风子，下笔便到乌丝栏。”别人临《兰亭》只得其面，他随手写去，却自然落在界格之内。[无锡博物院，杨凝式《韭花帖》](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

[《韭花帖》](../works/jiuhua-tie.md)是一封谢帖。他午睡起来，饿得厉害，恰有友人送来韭花：“当一叶报秋之初，乃韭花逞味之始。助其肥羜，实谓珍羞，充腹之馀，铭肌载切。”于是修状道谢。行楷，墨迹，麻纸本，高二十六厘米、宽二十八厘米，七行，六十余字；帖末只署“七月十一日”，没有年份。馆方的形容是“布局疏朗大气，用笔率性洒脱，时行时楷，时收时放”。一件只为道谢而写的短札，把字距行距都放开了，反而比郑重书写更经看。[无锡博物院，杨凝式《韭花帖》](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

这帖今天有三本之说。无锡博物院所藏是清宫内府本，乾隆在引首题“杨少师韭花帖妙迹”，入《石渠宝笈》，又收入《三希堂法帖》；另一本为罗振玉所藏，1945 年后不知所踪，很可能毁于战乱。两本真伪在书法界一直有争议，但徐邦达、谢稚柳两位对无锡这本的意见一致。清宫本于上世纪二十年代由溥仪盗出宫，后归无锡藏家薛氏，1982 年入藏无锡博物院。[无锡博物院，杨凝式《韭花帖》](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

无锡那篇文章另给了一个位置：清宫所藏历代法书浩若烟海，此帖被公认为“天下第五行书”——第一王羲之《兰亭序》，第二颜真卿《祭侄文稿》，第三苏轼《寒食帖》，第四王珣《伯远帖》，第五就是它。这个序列里，本库已有[《寒食帖》](../works/hanshi.md)、[《伯远帖》](../works/boyuan.md)与它自己三件。七行短札与三件长卷并坐，靠的不是字数：别人用几百字铺开的气势，它用六十余字收住。至于字数，馆方两篇文章一作六十三、一作六十五，读时不必急着定。[无锡博物院，《韭花帖》藏品条目](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/fab18864-9728-4d78-a7fe-37a67421ad03)

### 生平与身份

生卒、字号与籍贯待考——本库现有来源未记，只知生于唐末。无锡博物院记其历仕五代、官至太子太保、佯疯自晦而有「杨风子」雅号，上文已照录。[无锡博物院，杨凝式《韭花帖》](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于五代。

### 书体与师承

书体与师承待考——本库现有著录未逐字写明。无锡博物院记《韭花帖》为行楷，黄庭坚绝句称其「下笔便到乌丝栏」，是评其书而非记其师承。[无锡博物院，杨凝式《韭花帖》](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收[《韭花帖》](../works/jiuhua-tie.md)为一封谢帖，行楷墨迹麻纸本，七行，帖末只署「七月十一日」无年份。字数六十三、六十五两说并存，上文已记，不取其一。[无锡博物院，《韭花帖》藏品条目](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/fab18864-9728-4d78-a7fe-37a67421ad03)


### 可临之作

- [《韭花帖》](../works/jiuhua-tie.md)：杨凝式答谢友人馈赠韭花的行楷短札，纸本七行六十余字，末署日期无年份。

## English

Yang Ningshi was born at the end of the Tang and served at the courts of all five dynasties — Later Liang, Tang, Jin, Han and Zhou — rising to grand guardian of the heir apparent. He once feigned madness to keep himself out of harm, which earned him the nickname “Madman Yang”. Huang Tingjian wrote a quatrain about him alone: “The world all learns the face of the Lanting, and would trade mortal bones for a golden elixir; who would have thought that Madman Yang of Luoyang, setting down his brush, arrives at the silk grid.” Others copy the Lanting and get only its face; he writes offhand and lands inside the rule. [Wuxi Museum, Yang Ningshi’s Letter on Leek Flowers](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

The [Letter on Leek Flowers](../works/jiuhua-tie.md) is a thank-you note. He had woken from a nap and was very hungry when a friend sent leek flowers: “just as one leaf announces autumn, the leek flower begins to show its flavour; with the fat lamb it is truly a rare delicacy, and after filling the belly the gratitude is cut into the flesh.” So he wrote a note of thanks. Running-regular script, ink on hemp paper, 26 by 28 cm, seven lines, just over sixty characters; it closes with “the eleventh of the seventh month” and no year. The museum describes the layout as “open and generous, the brush easy and free, now running, now regular, now gathered, now released”. A short note written only to say thank you, with columns and lines let wide apart, turns out to bear looking at longer than a solemn piece of writing. [Wuxi Museum, Yang Ningshi’s Letter on Leek Flowers](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

Three versions are known today. The Wuxi Museum’s is the Qing palace copy, with Qianlong’s frontispiece, “the wondrous trace of Yang Shaoshi’s Leek Flower letter”; it was recorded in the Shiqu baoji and included in the Sanxitang fatie. The other belonged to Luo Zhenyu and has not been seen since 1945; it was probably destroyed in the fighting. Their authenticity has long been debated, though Xu Bangda and Xie Zhiliu agreed on the Wuxi copy. That copy was taken out of the palace by Puyi in the 1920s, passed to the Wuxi collector Xue, and entered the museum in 1982. [Wuxi Museum, Yang Ningshi’s Letter on Leek Flowers](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

The Wuxi article gives it one further position: among the vast imperial collection of calligraphy it is recognised as the “fifth running-script work under heaven” — first Wang Xizhi’s Lanting xu, second Yan Zhenqing’s Draft Elegy for a Nephew, third Su Shi’s Cold Food Poems, fourth Wang Xun’s Letter to Boyuan, fifth this. Three of that list are in this library: the Cold Food Poems, the Letter to Boyuan, and this one. A seven-line note sits beside three long scrolls, and not by count of characters: what the others unfurl over hundreds of characters, it gathers in just over sixty. As for the count itself, the museum’s two pages give sixty-three and sixty-five; it need not be settled before reading. [Wuxi Museum, collection entry for the Letter on Leek Flowers](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/fab18864-9728-4d78-a7fe-37a67421ad03)

### Life and identity

Dates, courtesy name and native place remain open; the sources here record only that he was born at the end of the Tang. The Wuxi Museum account of his service at all five dynasties, his office and the nickname "Madman Yang" is kept above. [Wuxi Museum, Yang Ningshi's Letter on Leek Flowers](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Five Dynasties.

### Scripts and models

Scripts and teacher lineage remain open; the cited catalogues do not state them in transcribed text. The museum records the letter as running-regular script, and Huang Tingjian's quatrain praises his brush, which is a judgement rather than a record of teachers. [Wuxi Museum, Yang Ningshi's Letter on Leek Flowers](https://www.wxmuseum.cn/News/Details/a77f8975-ec06-4367-ad8a-0818b08c535b)

### Works in this library

The [Letter on Leek Flowers](../works/jiuhua-tie.md) held here is a thank-you note in running-regular script, ink on hemp paper, seven lines, closing with a day but no year. The museum's two pages give sixty-three and sixty-five characters; both readings are kept and neither is settled, as noted above.

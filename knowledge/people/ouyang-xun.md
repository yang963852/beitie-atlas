# 欧阳询

## 中文

欧阳询（557—641），字信本，潭州临湘（今湖南长沙）人。他历陈、隋、唐三朝，贞观间官至太子率更令、弘文馆学士，封渤海县男，与虞世南、褚遂良、薛稷并称“初唐四家”。故宫博物院的小传叙其书迹，只举四种名碑：《虞恭公碑》《化度寺邕禅师塔铭》《九成宫醴泉铭》《皇甫诞碑》——四种都在本库。[故宫博物院，欧阳询行书张翰帖·欧阳询](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/234567.html)

四碑可以并看，先分清谁撰、谁书。[《九成宫醴泉铭》](../works/jiuchenggong-liquan-ming.md)的文字出自魏徵；[《虞恭公碑》](../works/yugong-gong-bei.md)出自岑文本，篆额四行十六字；[《皇甫诞碑》](../works/huangfu-dan-bei.md)出自于志宁。三碑的书丹都是欧阳询。同一个人写在三块石上，撰者却各不相同，读时分开记账即可。[故宫博物院，宋拓虞恭公温彦博碑](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228414.html) · [西安碑林博物馆，皇甫诞碑](https://beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2578) · [宝鸡市文物局，九成宫遗址答复函](https://wwj.baoji.gov.cn/col1892/col9955/202608/t20260821_1295837.html)

第四种[《化度寺邕禅师舍利塔铭》](../works/huadu-si.md)以拓本传世。吴湖帆所藏四种欧书宋拓，合称“四欧宝笈”，此铭居其一；展览说明中，策展人仲威逐一交代了这四种的书者与收藏。[澎湃新闻，四欧宝笈领衔唐碑善本大展](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_15167730)

碑刻之外还有一件墨迹可读。[《张翰帖》](../works/zhanghan.md)记西晋张翰见秋风起、思吴中菰菜鲈鱼而弃官归乡事。故宫著录为纸本行书，十一行九十八字，唐人所摹，并说是欧阳询存世四件墨迹之一。对开有宋徽宗瘦金书题跋，评此帖“笔法险劲，猛锐长驱”，又谓欧阳询“晚年笔力益刚劲，有执法面折庭争之风，孤峰崛起，四面削成”。隔着摹手看骨力，这段题跋是最省事的入口。[故宫博物院，欧阳询行书张翰帖](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/234567.html)

此帖原属《史事帖》。宋米芾《书史》记他曾于濮州李丞相家见十全帖，其后渐散佚，今存者只余数纸，此帖是其中一纸。[米芾](../people/mi-fu.md)读过的那一份，可从本库另存的米芾页接过去看。[故宫博物院，欧阳询行书张翰帖·史事帖](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/234567.html)

本库所收四碑一帖：[《九成宫醴泉铭》](../works/jiuchenggong-liquan-ming.md)、[《皇甫诞碑》](../works/huangfu-dan-bei.md)、[《化度寺邕禅师舍利塔铭》](../works/huadu-si.md)、[《虞恭公碑》](../works/yugong-gong-bei.md)与[《张翰帖》](../works/zhanghan.md)。故宫记《虞恭公碑》立春碑刻于贞观十一年（637）十月、岑文本撰、篆额四行十六字，本库照录。[故宫博物院，宋拓虞恭公温彦博碑](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228414.html)

## English

Ouyang Xun (557–641), courtesy name Xinben, was a native of Linxiang in Tan Prefecture, present-day Changsha in Hunan. He served through the Chen, Sui and Tang dynasties, rising under Taizong to Director of the Crown Prince’s Household and a scholar of the Hongwen Academy, and was enfeoffed as a baron of Bohai. The Palace Museum’s short biography pairs him with Yu Shinan, Chu Suiliang and Xue Ji as the Four Masters of the Early Tang, and names only four steles among his surviving works: the Yugong Gong Stele, the Huadu Temple Stupa Inscription, the Sweet Spring at Jiucheng Palace, and the Huangfu Dan Stele. All four are in this library. [Palace Museum, Ouyang Xun’s Zhang Han letter, biography of Ouyang Xun](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/234567.html)

The four can be read side by side once the composer and the writer are kept apart. Wei Zheng composed the text of the [Sweet Spring at Jiucheng Palace](../works/jiuchenggong-liquan-ming.md); Cen Wenben composed the [Yugong Gong Stele](../works/yugong-gong-bei.md), whose seal heading runs four lines of sixteen characters; Yu Zhining composed the [Huangfu Dan Stele](../works/huangfu-dan-bei.md). Ouyang wrote all three. One hand on three stones, three different authors — keep the accounts separate as you read. [Palace Museum, Song rubbing of the Yugong Gong Stele](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228414.html) · [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Huangfu Dan Stele](https://beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2578) · [Baoji Cultural Heritage Bureau, reply on the Jiucheng Palace site](https://wwj.baoji.gov.cn/col1892/col9955/202608/t20260821_1295837.html)

The fourth, the [Huadu Temple Stupa Inscription](../works/huadu-si.md), survives as rubbings. Wu Hufan’s four Song rubbings of Ouyang’s writing were known together as the Four Ouyang Treasures, and this inscription is one of them; the exhibition account has the curator Zhong Wei going through the writer and the collecting history of each. [The Paper, exhibition of the Four Ouyang Treasures](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_15167730)

Beyond the stones there is one sheet of ink to read. The [Zhang Han letter](../works/zhanghan.md) tells how Zhang Han, seeing the autumn wind rise, thought of the water-shield soup and perch of his native Wu and gave up office to go home. The Palace Museum catalogues it as ink on paper, running script, eleven lines and ninety-eight characters, a Tang tracing, and calls it one of four surviving examples of Ouyang’s brushwork. On the facing leaf is a colophon in Emperor Huizong’s slender-gold script, which judges the letter “vigorous in method, fierce and driving”, and says of Ouyang that “in late years his stroke grew firmer, with the manner of one who holds the law and argues to the emperor’s face, a lone peak rising, cut on four sides.” To read bone through a copyist’s hand, that colophon is the shortest way in. [Palace Museum, Ouyang Xun’s Zhang Han letter](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/234567.html)

The letter belonged to the Shishi Tie, a gathering of Ouyang’s accounts of earlier figures. Mi Fu, in his Shishi, records seeing a complete set of ten at the house of a former chancellor in Puzhou; the set later scattered, and only a few sheets remain, this among them. The copy Mi Fu read can be reached from the [Mi Fu](../people/mi-fu.md) page kept here. [Palace Museum, Ouyang Xun’s Zhang Han letter, entry on the Shishi Tie](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/234567.html)

### Works in this library

The library holds four steles and one letter: the [Sweet Spring at Jiucheng Palace](../works/jiuchenggong-liquan-ming.md), [Huangfu Dan Stele](../works/huangfu-dan-bei.md), [Huadu Temple Stupa Inscription](../works/huadu-si.md), [Yugong Gong Stele](../works/yugong-gong-bei.md) and [Zhang Han letter](../works/zhanghan.md). The Palace Museum dates the Yugong Gong stone to the tenth month of 637, names Cen Wenben as composer and records a seal heading of four lines of sixteen characters; this is recorded as stated. [Palace Museum, Song rubbing of the Yugong Gong Stele](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/228414.html)

# 蔡襄

## 中文

蔡襄（1012—1067），字君谟，兴化仙游（今属福建）人，举进士，在泉州、福州、杭州等地做过官，有《蔡端明文集》，《宋史》有传。故宫的小传把一句话放在最前面：他的书法“在当时享有很高的声誉，被推为本朝第一”，又与苏轼、黄庭坚、米芾并称“宋四家”。四家并称是后来的事，“本朝第一”却是他活着时听到的话。[故宫博物院，蔡襄行书自书诗卷·蔡襄](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

[《自书诗卷》](../works/cai-zishu.md)的来历，故宫记得很具体：皇祐二年（1050），蔡襄罢福建转运使，召还汴京修起居注，于是从福州一路北行，走了半年多；沿途见闻有感于怀的都写成诗，卷中五言、七言共十一首，书写时间当在诗成之后不久，时年约四十。纸本，三接纸，纵二十八点二厘米、横二百二十一点二厘米，行书七十三行、八百八十四字。半年的路收进十一首诗，再收进两米多长的一卷纸——这不是抄诗，是一段路被压平了。[故宫博物院，蔡襄行书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

故宫对它的判断是“因属个人诗稿，无意求工”，所以“笔致飘逸流畅，点画婉转精美”，显出中年“清健圆润”的面貌。可卷里已经有人在读它：第三首诗题下有一行小字“此一篇极有古人风格”，据卷后杨时跋，是欧阳修写的。诗成不久，另一位大手笔就在上面批了一句。近代朱文钧的跋说得更细：“此册行楷略备，无不臻美。其婉约处极似虞永兴，而温栗不减柳谏议。盖其能博采约举以自成一家书派者。”一句里点了两家——虞世南的婉约、柳公权的温栗，本库有[《孔子庙堂碑》](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md)与[《玄秘塔碑》](../works/xuanmi-ta-bei.md)可以对看。[故宫博物院，蔡襄行书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

稿与帖的分界在后头。此卷经贾似道、梁清标、嘉庆内府递藏，著录于《珊瑚网》《吴氏书画记》《平生壮观》《石渠宝笈三编》，又刻入《秋碧堂》《经训堂》《玉虹鉴真》诸帖。刻进帖里，一件不能复制的墨迹就变成可以反复临写的范本，同一卷字从此有了墨迹与拓本两种读法。所以读它的顺序不妨倒过来：先记住这是赶路途中随手写的十一首诗，再看那些笔画里哪一处是当日的速度，哪一处是后世的刀。[故宫博物院，蔡襄行书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

## English

Cai Xiang (1012–1067), courtesy name Junmo, a native of Xianyou in Xinghua (modern Fujian), passed the examinations, served in Quanzhou, Fuzhou and Hangzhou, and left the Cai Duanming collection; the Song History carries his biography. The Palace Museum’s note puts one sentence first: his calligraphy “enjoyed a high reputation in his own day and was ranked first of the dynasty”, and he is named with Su Shi, Huang Tingjian and Mi Fu as the Four Masters of the Song. The fourfold name came later; “first of the dynasty” was said while he was alive to hear it. [Palace Museum, Cai Xiang’s poem scroll, biography](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

The museum records the occasion of the [Scroll of Poems in the Author’s Hand](../works/cai-zishu.md) precisely: in the second year of Huangyou (1050) Cai was relieved of the post of Fujian transport commissioner and recalled to the capital as diarist, so he went north from Fuzhou and was more than half a year on the road; whatever moved him on the way became a poem, and the scroll holds eleven of them in five- and seven-character lines, written not long after they were made, when he was about forty. Paper, three joined sheets, 28.2 by 221.2 cm, running script, seventy-three lines and 884 characters. Half a year of road pressed into eleven poems, then into a scroll over two metres long: this is not poems copied out, it is a journey flattened. [Palace Museum, Cai Xiang’s poem scroll](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

The museum’s judgement is that the scroll was “a personal draft, with no thought of refinement”, hence the “fluent, drifting brush and the graceful, turning strokes”, the “clear and rounded” manner of his middle years. Yet someone was already reading it inside the scroll: under the title of the third poem stands a line in small characters, “this one has very much the air of the ancients”, which the colophon by Yang Shi at the end attributes to Ouyang Xiu. Soon after the poems were finished, another great hand had written a comment on them. The modern colophon by Zhu Wenjun goes further: “This album has both running and regular script, and nothing in it falls short of the excellent. Where it is graceful it comes very close to Yu Yongxing; where it is warm and firm it yields nothing to Liu Jianyi. This is how he gathered widely, chose the essential, and made a school of his own.” Two names in one sentence — the grace of Yu Shinan and the warmth and firmness of Liu Gongquan; this library holds the [Confucius Temple Stele](../works/kongzi-miaotang-bei.md) and the [Xuanmi Pagoda Stele](../works/xuanmi-ta-bei.md) to set beside it. [Palace Museum, Cai Xiang’s poem scroll](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

The line between draft and model comes later. The scroll passed through Jia Sidao, Liang Qingbiao and the Jiaqing palace collection, was recorded in the Shanhu wang, the Wushi shuhua ji, the Pingsheng zhuangguan and the third series of the Shiqu baoji, and was carved into the Qiubi tang, Jingxun tang and Yuhong jianzhen modelbooks. Once carved into a modelbook, an unrepeatable manuscript becomes a copybook that can be written from again and again: one scroll, two ways of reading it, as ink and as rubbing. So the order might be reversed — remember first that these are eleven poems written on the road, then look at the strokes and ask which passage is the speed of that day and which is a later knife. [Palace Museum, Cai Xiang’s poem scroll](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228526.html)

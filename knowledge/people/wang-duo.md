# 王铎

## 中文

王铎（1592—1652），字觉斯，号嵩樵、烟潭渔叟、痴仙道人，孟津（今洛阳孟津县）人。明天启二年（1622）进士，累官礼部尚书、东阁大学士。书法师钟繇、二王、颜真卿、米芾，尤得力于《淳化阁帖》，既浸淫古法又有所创新，最擅行、草，刻有《拟山园帖》《琅华馆帖》，《清史稿》有传。[故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷·王铎](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

崇祯十二年（1639）正月，他四十七岁，写下[《行草书自书诗卷》](../works/wangduo-zishu-shi.md)，书自作诗六首。卷为纸本，纵二十六点九厘米，横一百六十六厘米；起首作「君宣年家兄翁判南康，赠以六首」，末款「己卯正月弟王铎具草」，左钤「王铎之印」「烟潭渔叟」。[故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

近人马宗霍说：「明人草书，无不纵笔以取势者，觉斯则纵而能敛，故不极势而势若不尽，非力有余者，未易语此。」这卷正可拿来验这句话：笔势一路连绵，却始终没有放尽，收在行气里。[故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

### 生平与身份

上文所记生卒、字号、籍贯与历官沿用原文，但本库来源的「简介」未逐字转录故宫小传，故不据此增添新的传记数值。[故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于清；馆方条目朝代作「清」而款识为己卯（明崇祯十二年），本库按书写年份系于明，此出入上文已记，不取其一。

## English

Wang Duo (1592–1652), courtesy name Juesi, style names Songqiao, Yantan Yusou and Chixian Daoren, was a native of Mengjin, modern Mengjin county in Luoyang. He passed the examination in 1622, the second year of Tianqi, and rose to minister of rites and grand secretary of the East Pavilion. He studied calligraphy under Zhong Yao, the two Wangs, Yan Zhenqing and Mi Fu, drew especially on the Chunhua ge tie, steeped himself in the old methods and yet innovated; he was best at running and cursive script, and carved the Nishan yuan tie and Langhua guan tie. The Qing shi gao carries his biography. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems, biography](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

In the first month of 1639, at forty-seven, he wrote the [Scroll of His Own Poems in Running-Cursive Script](../works/wangduo-zishu-shi.md), carrying six of his own poems. It is ink on paper, 26.9 by 166 cm; it opens “for my elder brother of the same examination year, Judge of Nankang, six poems presented”, closes “respectfully drafted by your younger brother Wang Duo, first month of jimao”, and carries the seals “Wang Duo’s seal” and “Fisherman of the Misty Pool” at the left. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

The modern scholar Ma Zonghuo wrote: “Ming men’s cursive all stretch the brush to seize momentum; Juesi stretches and can also hold back, so he never exhausts the momentum and yet it seems inexhaustible — this is not something a man of less than full strength could say.” The scroll tests that sentence: the brush runs on and on, yet never quite lets go, held inside the column rhythm. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

### Life and identity

The dates, courtesy name, native place and offices above are kept; the "brief introduction" in the listed source does not transcribe the museum biography verbatim, so no further biographical values are added. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo's scroll of his own poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Qing, while the museum entry's Qing dating and the jimao signature (1639, Ming Chongzhen twelfth year) differ; this library dates by the writing year, and both readings were noted above without preference.

### Scripts and models

The account above — studying Zhong Yao, the two Wangs, Yan Zhenqing and Mi Fu, drawing especially on the Chunhua ge tie — is from the museum biography. Ma Zonghuo's phrase "stretches and can also hold back" is a judgement on his cursive. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo's scroll of his own poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

# 王铎

## 中文

王铎（1592—1652），字觉斯，一字觉之，号十樵、石樵、嵩樵、雪山、痴庵、烟潭渔叟等，河南孟津人。明天启二年（1622）进士，殿试三甲第五十八名，与倪元璐、黄道周等同选翰林院庶吉士。[故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷·王铎](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html) [上海博物馆，清 王铎 草书唐人诗卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00166295) 崇祯年间官至礼部尚书；崇祯十三年（1640）欲赴南京礼部尚书任，因战乱避居怀州。崇祯十七年（1644）福王朱由崧监国南京，召为东阁大学士，位次辅。弘光元年（1645）五月南京城陷，他与钱谦益等降清。入清后于顺治三年（1646）正月授礼部尚书、充《明史》副总裁，顺治六年（1649）授礼部左侍郎、充《太宗文皇帝实录》副总裁，顺治八年（1651）奉旨祭告华山、峨眉，归途染疾，次年病逝故里，享年六十一，谥文安。乾隆四十一年（1776），清廷修《贰臣传》，将他列入。[故宫博物院，王铎及其书画艺术谈略](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf)

王铎的书法以行草为长。他自述十五岁起钻研《集王圣教序》，又说「予书独宗羲献，即唐宋诸家皆发源羲献」；馆方文章总结他小楷源自钟繇，大楷宗颜真卿、柳公权，行书最重米芾，临古主要取王羲之、王献之，旁及王筠、郗愔。故宫小传则记他师钟繇、二王、颜真卿、米芾，尤得力于《淳化阁帖》，最擅行、草，刻有《拟山园帖》《琅华馆帖》。[故宫博物院，王铎及其书画艺术谈略](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷·王铎](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

黄道周在《书品论》里说：「行草近推王觉斯。觉斯方盛年，看其五十自化。」傅山《霜红龛书论》说：「王铎四十年前字极力造作，四十年后无意合拍，遂能大家。」龚鼎孳《题拟山园帖后》称：「文安公书法妙天下，真得晋人三昧。」近人马宗霍则说：「明人草书，无不纵笔以取势者，觉斯则纵而能敛，故不极势而势若不尽，非力有余者，未易语此。」[故宫博物院，王铎及其书画艺术谈略](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

### 生平与身份

本页所记生卒、字号、籍贯与历官，以故宫研究文章为主，兼取故宫藏品页与上海博物馆藏品页。需要说明一处异说：该文正文括注天启二年为「一六二三年」，而故宫藏品页与上海博物馆均记天启二年（1622）；本库取 1622，异说并存。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于清；其 1639 年所书《行草书自书诗卷》则按书写年份系于明，详见该作品页的待考。[故宫博物院，王铎及其书画艺术谈略](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html) [上海博物馆，清 王铎 草书唐人诗卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00166295)

### 书体与师承

王铎临古，主要取晋人，尤重王羲之、王献之父子，兼及王筠、郗愔；小楷出自钟繇，大楷宗颜真卿、柳公权，行书最重米芾。他自述十五岁钻研《集王圣教序》，五十八岁时又说：「予书独宗羲献，即唐宋诸家皆发源羲献，人自不察耳。」馆方文章引王连起之说，认为他「认准羲献」是认准了书法艺术发展的方向；又引黄道周「看其五十自化」、傅山「四十年后无意合拍，遂能大家」两语，以说明其书风在五十岁前后由刻意求工转向自然。故宫小传另记其刻《拟山园帖》《琅华馆帖》。[故宫博物院，王铎及其书画艺术谈略](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷·王铎](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收五件，正好排出他从崇祯到顺治的几段面貌。

[《行书送友诗轴》](../works/wangduo-songyou-shi.md)书于崇祯九年（1636）秋，绢本，纵179.5厘米、横58厘米，时王铎任南京翰林院掌院学士。馆方称其点画沉厚、笔势开张，可见颜、米二家的影响。[故宫博物院，新理异态展·王铎行书送友诗轴](https://www.dpm.org.cn/show/236325.html) 尺寸与绢本另见同馆文章。[故宫博物院，王铎及其书画艺术谈略](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf)

[《行草书自书诗卷》](../works/wangduo-zishu-shi.md)书于崇祯十二年（1639）正月，纸本，纵26.9厘米、横166厘米，时年四十七，书自作诗六首。馆方记其款署「己卯正月弟王铎具草」，钤「王铎之印」「烟潭渔叟」；马宗霍「纵而能敛」之评，正可拿来读这卷。[故宫博物院，王铎行草书自书诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

[《赠张抱一草书诗卷》](../works/wangduo-zhangbaoyi.md)书于崇祯十五年（1642），绫本接纸本，纵32.0厘米、横658.0厘米，时年五十一，尚未降清。馆方称其笔致自由阔达。[东京国立博物馆 ColBase，草書詩書巻](https://colbase.nich.go.jp/collection_items/tnm/TB-1257?locale=ja) 怀州、五十一岁与绫本接纸本之说，另见日本文化厅文化遗产数据库。[文化庁 文化遺産オンライン，草書詩書巻](https://online.bunka.go.jp/db/heritages/detail/519383)

[《草书唐人诗卷》](../works/wangduo-dufu-shi.md)书于清顺治三年（1646），纸本，时年五十五，录杜甫诗七首及王湾、祖咏、綦毋潜诗各一首。馆方称其用笔凝练俊健、使转纵横。[上海博物馆，清 王铎 草书唐人诗卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00166295)

[《行书临汝帖轴》](../works/wangduo-linru-tie.md)书于同年八月，绫本，纵237厘米、横53.4厘米，临《汝帖》中司马曜、陈伯智、阮籍三帖。馆方指出此作对原帖有脱字，而笔法、结构、布白、墨法既渊源有自又自出匠心，可见其学书方法。[故宫博物院，行书临汝帖轴](https://ggzl.dpm.org.cn/pages/exhibit_works/details?id=12680)

## English

Wang Duo (1592–1652), courtesy name Juesi, also Juezhi, styled Shiqiao (十樵), Shiqiao (石樵), Songqiao, Xueshan, Chi’an and Yantan Yusou, among other names, was a native of Mengjin, Henan. He passed the examination in 1622, the second year of Tianqi, placed fifty-eighth in the third class, and was selected with Ni Yuanlu, Huang Daozhou and others as a Hanlin bachelor. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems, biography of Wang Duo](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html) [Shanghai Museum, Wang Duo’s cursive-script scroll of Tang poems](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00166295) During the Chongzhen reign he rose to minister of rites; in 1640, the thirteenth year, he intended to take up the post of minister of rites at Nanjing but was driven by war to stay at Huaizhou. In 1644, the seventeenth year, Prince Fu, Zhu Yousong, assumed the regency at Nanjing and summoned him as grand secretary of the East Pavilion, second in rank. In the fifth month of 1645, the first year of Hongguang, Nanjing fell, and he surrendered to the Qing together with Qian Qianyi and others. Under the Qing he was appointed minister of rites and vice-director of the Ming History project in the first month of 1646; in 1649 he was appointed left vice-minister of rites and vice-director of the Taizong shilu; in 1651 he was sent by imperial order to sacrifice at Mount Hua and Mount Emei, fell ill on the return journey, and died at home the following year at sixty-one, with the posthumous name Wen’an. In 1776, the forty-first year of Qianlong, the court compiling the Erchen zhuan included him among the officials who served two dynasties. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo and his painting and calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf)

Wang Duo was strongest in running and cursive script. He said he had studied the Shengjiao xu assembled from Wang Xizhi’s characters from the age of fifteen, and that “my calligraphy takes Xi and Xian alone as its source; the Tang and Song masters all spring from Xi and Xian.” The museum article summarizes his models: small regular script from Zhong Yao, large regular script from Yan Zhenqing and Liu Gongquan, running script above all from Mi Fu; in studying the old masters he drew mainly on Wang Xizhi and Wang Xianzhi, with Wang Yun and Xi Yin as secondary. The Palace Museum’s biography records that he studied Zhong Yao, the two Wangs, Yan Zhenqing and Mi Fu, drew especially on the Chunhua ge tie, was best at running and cursive, and carved the Nishan yuan tie and Langhua guan tie. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo and his painting and calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems, biography of Wang Duo](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

Huang Daozhou wrote in his “Shupin lun”: “In running and cursive the nearest is Wang Juesi. Juesi is in his prime; watch how he transforms at fifty.” Fu Shan wrote in the Shuanghongkan shulun: “Wang Duo’s characters before forty were forced; after forty, without trying to fit, he became a master.” Gong Dingzi, in a colophon to the Nishan yuan tie, said: “The calligraphy of Lord Wen’an is wondrous under heaven, truly attaining the samadhi of the Jin.” The modern scholar Ma Zonghuo wrote: “Ming men’s cursive all stretch the brush to seize momentum; Juesi stretches and can also hold back, so he never exhausts the momentum and yet it seems inexhaustible — this is not something a man of less than full strength could say.” [Palace Museum, Wang Duo and his painting and calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

### Life and identity

The dates, courtesy names, native place and offices above follow the Palace Museum article, with the museum object page and the Shanghai Museum page. One discrepancy should be noted: the article’s text glosses the second year of Tianqi as 1623, while the Palace Museum object page and the Shanghai Museum both give 1622; this library takes 1622 and keeps both readings. By `dynastySource` the page places him in the Qing; his 1639 Scroll of His Own Poems is filed by writing year under the Ming, as explained on that work’s page. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo and his painting and calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html) [Shanghai Museum, Wang Duo’s cursive-script scroll of Tang poems](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00166295)

### Scripts and models

Wang Duo’s study of the old masters drew mainly on the Jin, above all on Wang Xizhi and Wang Xianzhi, with Wang Yun and Xi Yin as secondary; his small regular script came from Zhong Yao, his large regular script from Yan Zhenqing and Liu Gongquan, and his running script above all from Mi Fu. He said he had studied the Shengjiao xu assembled from Wang Xizhi’s characters from fifteen, and at fifty-eight: “My calligraphy takes Xi and Xian alone as its source; the Tang and Song masters all spring from Xi and Xian, which people do not examine.” The museum article cites Wang Lianqi’s view that in “choosing Xi and Xian” he chose the right direction for calligraphy, and quotes Huang Daozhou’s “watch how he transforms at fifty” and Fu Shan’s “after forty, without trying to fit, he became a master” to describe the shift from deliberate craft to naturalness around fifty. The Palace Museum biography adds that he carved the Nishan yuan tie and Langhua guan tie. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo and his painting and calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf) [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems, biography of Wang Duo](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

### Works in this library

The five works held here trace several phases from Chongzhen to Shunzhi.

The [Poem on Sending a Friend in Running Script](../works/wangduo-songyou-shi.md) was written in the autumn of 1636, on silk, 179.5 by 58 cm, when Wang Duo was chancellor of the Nanjing Hanlin Academy. The museum describes its strokes as deep and heavy, its momentum open, showing the influence of Yan Zhenqing and Mi Fu. [Palace Museum, exhibition of five late-Ming and early-Qing masters, Wang Duo’s poem on sending a friend](https://www.dpm.org.cn/show/236325.html) The dimensions and silk support are also given by the museum article. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo and his painting and calligraphy](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2019/05/13/u5cd90d3d47fb0.pdf)

The [Scroll of His Own Poems in Running-Cursive Script](../works/wangduo-zishu-shi.md) was written in the first month of 1639, on paper, 26.9 by 166 cm, at forty-seven, carrying six of his own poems. The museum records the signature “respectfully drafted by your younger brother Wang Duo, first month of jimao” and the seals “Wang Duo’s seal” and “Fisherman of the Misty Pool”; Ma Zonghuo’s phrase “stretches and can also hold back” can be tested on this scroll. [Palace Museum, Wang Duo’s scroll of his own poems](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228265.html)

The [Poems for Zhang Baoyi in Cursive Script](../works/wangduo-zhangbaoyi.md) was written in 1642, on silk with paper joined at the end, 32.0 by 658.0 cm, at fifty-one, before his surrender to the Qing. The museum describes the brushwork as free and expansive. [Tokyo National Museum, ColBase, cursive-script poetry scroll](https://colbase.nich.go.jp/collection_items/tnm/TB-1257?locale=ja) The details of Huaizhou, the age of fifty-one and the silk-and-paper joining are also given by the Agency for Cultural Affairs heritage database. [Agency for Cultural Affairs, Cultural Heritage Online, cursive-script poetry scroll](https://online.bunka.go.jp/db/heritages/detail/519383)

The [Poems by Tang Poets in Cursive Script](../works/wangduo-dufu-shi.md) was written in 1646, the third year of Shunzhi, on paper, at fifty-five, carrying seven poems by Du Fu and one each by Wang Wan, Zu Yong and Qiwu Qian. The museum describes the brush as condensed and vigorous, the turns free and sweeping. [Shanghai Museum, Wang Duo’s cursive-script scroll of Tang poems](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/m/article/id/CI00166295)

The [Copy of the Ru Modelbook in Running Script](../works/wangduo-linru-tie.md) was written in the eighth month of the same year, on silk, 237 by 53.4 cm, copying three pieces by Sima Yao, Chen Bozhi and Ruan Ji from the Ru modelbook. The museum notes that characters are omitted from the originals, while brush method, structure, spacing and ink are both rooted in the sources and the writer’s own, showing his way of studying calligraphy. [Palace Museum, hanging scroll of a copy of the Ru modelbook](https://ggzl.dpm.org.cn/pages/exhibit_works/details?id=12680)

### Works to copy

- [Poem on Sending a Friend in Running Script](../works/wangduo-songyou-shi.md): a five-character regulated verse on silk from 1636, useful for seeing the Yan and Mi influence in his middle-period running script.
- [Poems for Zhang Baoyi in Cursive Script](../works/wangduo-zhangbaoyi.md): a long silk scroll of 1642, written at fifty-one, useful for seeing the continuity and column division of his cursive.
- [Poems by Tang Poets in Cursive Script](../works/wangduo-dufu-shi.md): a paper scroll of 1646, written at fifty-five, useful for seeing the condensation and turns of his late cursive.
- [Copy of the Ru Modelbook in Running Script](../works/wangduo-linru-tie.md): a 1646 copy of three pieces from the Ru modelbook, useful for seeing how he studied the old masters without copying their outward form.

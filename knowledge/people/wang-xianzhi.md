# 王献之

## 中文

王献之（344—386），字子敬，小名官奴，王羲之第七子，官至中书令，人称「王大令」，与父并称「二王」。故宫博物院所附小传记其传世书迹有《鸭头丸帖》《中秋帖》《玉版十三行》等。[故宫，东山松帖页所附小传](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229389.html)

本库所收，没有一件可定为他的手笔：《鸭头丸帖》为唐摹本，《东山松帖》为墨迹页而馆方未论摹本，《万岁通天帖》卷中存其书翰；《中秋帖》旧题其书，故宫指出纸料与用笔与晋人书迹有别；《洛神赋十三行》所存为玉版刻石。读这几条时，可把「原书」与「存世一本」分开记。

### 生平与身份

本卷已有的生卒、字号与家世沿用上文，本库现有来源未逐字转录故宫所附小传，故不据此增添新的传记事实。[故宫，东山松帖页所附小传](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229389.html)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于东晋。

### 书体与师承

书体与师承待考——本库现有著录未逐字写明。上海博物馆记《鸭头丸帖》为唐摹本、存部分宣和裱，不曾论及他的师承。[上海博物馆，鸭头丸帖](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/museum/dongqichang/detail_2.html?id=5)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收有[《洛神赋十三行》](../works/luoshen-shisanhang.md)、[《中秋帖》](../works/zhongqiu.md)、[《万岁通天帖》](../works/wansui-tongtian-tie.md)、[《鸭头丸帖》](../works/yatouwan.md)与[《东山松帖》](../works/dongshansong.md)。馆方对这些作品有的是传本、有的是旧题、有的是刻石，各条分记，不合并成一件确定的手笔。


### 可临之作

- [《洛神赋十三行》](../works/luoshen-shisanhang.md)：王献之《洛神赋》小楷，传世为南宋贾似道所刻玉版十三行，今藏首都博物馆。
- [《中秋帖》](../works/zhongqiu.md)：旧题王献之的行草三行帖，故宫指出纸料用笔异于晋人书迹，今多认为宋人临写。
- [《万岁通天帖》](../works/wansui-tongtian-tie.md)：697年弘文馆勾填的王氏一门书信卷，今存七人十通，藏辽宁省博物馆，不是王氏真迹。
- [《鸭头丸帖》](../works/yatouwan.md)：王献之《鸭头丸帖》的纸本唐摹本，存部分宣和裱，有董其昌等跋。
- [《东山松帖》](../works/dongshansong.md)：王献之的行草通信札断片，纸本有四字磨灭，馆方未说明是否摹本。

## English

Wang Xianzhi (344-386), courtesy name Zijing, seventh son of Wang Xizhi, rose to the Palace Secretariat and was known as the Great Minister Wang; father and son are called the Two Wangs. The Palace Museum's note lists the Duck-Head Pill Letter, the Mid-Autumn Letter and the Jade-version Thirteen Lines among his transmitted works. [Palace Museum, biography attached to the Dongshan Pine leaf](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229389.html)

Nothing in this library can be called his own hand: the Duck-Head Pill Letter is a Tang tracing copy, the Dongshan Pine leaf is an ink leaf whose copy status the museum leaves open, and one of his letters survives inside the Wan Sui Tong Tian scroll. The Mid-Autumn Letter is traditionally his, but the palace museum notes that its paper and brushwork differ from Jin practice. The Thirteen Lines survive as a stone engraving. In each record the original writing and the surviving witness are kept apart.

### Life and identity

The dates, courtesy name and family already given above are kept; the sources listed for this page do not transcribe the museum biography verbatim, so no further biographical facts are added here. [Palace Museum, biography attached to the Dongshan Pine leaf](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/229389.html). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Eastern Jin.

### Scripts and models

Scripts and teacher lineage remain open; the cited catalogues do not state them in transcribed text. The Shanghai Museum records the Duck-Head Pill Letter as a Tang tracing copy with part of its Xuanhe mounting, without discussing his teachers. [Shanghai Museum, Duck-Head Pill Letter](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/museum/dongqichang/detail_2.html?id=5)

### Works in this library

The library holds the [Thirteen Lines of the Nymph of the Luo River](../works/luoshen-shisanhang.md), [Mid-Autumn Letter](../works/zhongqiu.md), [Wan Sui Tong Tian scroll](../works/wansui-tongtian-tie.md), [Duck-Head Pill Letter](../works/yatouwan.md) and [Dongshan Pine leaf](../works/dongshansong.md). The museums catalogue these variously as transmitted copies, traditional attributions or stone engravings; each is kept as its own record rather than merged into one settled hand.

### Works to copy

- [Thirteen Lines from the Goddess of the Luo](../works/luoshen-shisanhang.md): Wang Xianzhi’s small-regular-script Rhapsody on the Goddess of the Luo, surviving as the Jade-Version Thirteen Lines engraved for Jia Sidao in the Southern Song and now at the Capital Museum.
- [Mid-Autumn Letter](../works/zhongqiu.md): A three-line running-cursive letter traditionally attributed to Wang Xianzhi, whose paper and brushwork differ from Jin practice and are now often read as a Song copy.
- [Model Letters of the Wan Sui Tong Tian Year](../works/wansui-tongtian-tie.md): A scroll traced and ink-filled by the Hongwen Academy in 697, preserving ten letters by seven Wang-family hands at the Liaoning Provincial Museum rather than Wang autographs.
- [Duck-Head Pill Letter](../works/yatouwan.md): A Tang tracing copy on paper of Wang Xianzhi’s Duck-Head Pill Letter, retaining part of its Xuanhe mounting and later colophons including Dong Qichang’s.
- [Letter on the Dongshan Pines](../works/dongshansong.md): A paper fragment of a running-cursive letter attributed to Wang Xianzhi, with four characters effaced and its copy status left open by the museum.

# 怀素

## 中文

### 生平与身份

怀素（737—799），俗姓钱，字藏真，湖南永州人，唐代书法家，以「狂草」出名，与张旭并称「颠张醉素」。[西安碑林博物馆，怀素草书千字文](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于唐。另需说明：上博专题展页的检索摘要作「长沙人」，与碑林「湖南永州人」不同，摘要非页面实读，本库不取。

### 书体与师承

碑林记其以狂草出名、与张旭并称「颠张醉素」，并评所刻《千字文》「狂放不羁，纵横自如，奔逸中有清秀之神，狂放中有淳穆之气」；上博称《苦笋帖》为「唐代草书大家怀素的传世亲笔墨迹之一」。[西安碑林博物馆，怀素草书千字文](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631) · [上海博物馆，草书苦笋帖卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/P00002749)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收[《自叙帖》](../works/zixu.md)为台北故宫著录的墨迹卷；该馆数位典藏页本次多次无法打开，故本页不逐字引其内容，作品细节留在作品条目与待考中。另有上博《苦笋帖》绢本，纵二十五点一厘米、横十二厘米，全文十四字，馆方称为传世亲笔墨迹之一。[上海博物馆，草书苦笋帖卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/P00002749)

## English

### Life and identity

Huaisu (737–799), whose secular surname was Qian and courtesy name Zangzhen, was a native of Yongzhou in Hunan and a Tang calligrapher famed for wild cursive, paired with Zhang Xu as "mad Zhang, drunk Su." [Xi'an Beilin Museum, Huaisu's Thousand Character Classic](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Tang. A search summary of a Shanghai Museum exhibition page calls him a native of Changsha, differing from the Beilin's Yongzhou; the summary was not read on the page and is not used.

### Scripts and models

The Beilin records his fame for wild cursive and the pairing with Zhang Xu, and describes the carved Thousand Character Classic as "unrestrained, moving freely, with a clear and elegant spirit inside the wildness." The Shanghai Museum calls the Kusun tie "one of the surviving autograph ink traces of the great Tang cursive master Huaisu." [Xi'an Beilin Museum, Thousand Character Classic](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631) · [Shanghai Museum, Kusun tie](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/P00002749)

### Works in this library

The [Zixu tie](../works/zixu.md) held here is the ink scroll catalogued by the National Palace Museum; its digital record could not be opened during this check, so this page does not quote it verbatim and leaves the details to the work record. The Shanghai Museum's Kusun tie is ink on silk, 25.1 by 12 cm, fourteen characters, called by the museum one of his surviving autograph ink traces. [Shanghai Museum, Kusun tie](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/P00002749)

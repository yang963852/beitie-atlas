# 怀素

## 中文

### 生平与身份

怀素（737—799），俗姓钱，字藏真，唐代书法家，以「狂草」出名，与张旭并称「颠张醉素」。籍贯有两说：西安碑林博物馆作「湖南永州人」，上海博物馆现行《苦笋帖》藏品页作「长沙人」，本库并列而不择一。[西安碑林博物馆，怀素草书千字文](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631) · [上海博物馆，唐怀素草书苦笋帖卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000593)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于唐。

### 书体与师承

碑林记其以狂草出名、与张旭并称「颠张醉素」，并评所刻《千字文》「狂放不羁，纵横自如，奔逸中有清秀之神，狂放中有淳穆之气」；上博称《苦笋帖》为唐代草书大家怀素的传世亲笔墨迹之一，现行藏品页更将其书体字段定为「草书」。[西安碑林博物馆，怀素草书千字文](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631) · [上海博物馆，草书苦笋帖卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/P00002749) · [上海博物馆，唐怀素草书苦笋帖卷](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000593)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收[《自叙帖》](../works/zixu.md)为台北故宫著录的草书纸本墨迹卷，馆方创作时间字段作大历十二年（777），本幅28.3×755公分；前六行是否补书，李东阳与高士奇题跋意见不同，作品条目并列保留。[国立故宫博物院，唐怀素自叙帖卷](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/14901?dep=P)

另有上博[《苦笋帖》](../works/kusun.md)，现行藏品页定唐代、丝绢、草书，帖心25.1×12厘米；旧专题页录全文十四字，并记经宋宣和、绍兴内府递藏。两处馆方页面均未给确切书写年份。[上海博物馆现行藏品页](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000593) · [上海博物馆旧专题页](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/resource/winchance/n11.html)


### 可临之作

- [《自叙帖》](../works/zixu.md)：怀素自述生平与书学经历的草书帖，馆方系于大历十二年（777）。
- [《苦笋帖》](../works/kusun.md)：上海博物馆所藏唐代怀素草书丝绢短札，帖心25.1×12厘米；确切书写年份不详。

## English

### Life and identity

Huaisu (737–799), whose secular surname was Qian and courtesy name was Zangzhen, was a Tang calligrapher famed for wild cursive and paired with Zhang Xu as "mad Zhang, drunk Su." Two native-place accounts are kept: the Xi'an Beilin Museum calls him a native of Yongzhou in Hunan, while the Shanghai Museum's current *Kusun tie* entry calls him a native of Changsha. Neither is preferred here. [Xi'an Beilin Museum, Huaisu's Thousand Character Classic](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631) · [Shanghai Museum, Tang Huaisu, Cursive Letter on Bitter Bamboo Shoots](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000593). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Tang.

### Scripts and models

The Beilin records his fame for wild cursive and the pairing with Zhang Xu, and describes the carved Thousand Character Classic as "unrestrained, moving freely, with a clear and elegant spirit inside the wildness." Shanghai Museum calls the Kusun tie one of the surviving autograph ink traces of the great Tang cursive master Huaisu; its current object catalogue explicitly classifies the script as cursive. [Xi'an Beilin Museum, Thousand Character Classic](https://www.beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2631) · [Shanghai Museum, Kusun tie](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/P00002749) · [Shanghai Museum, Tang Huaisu, Cursive Letter on Bitter Bamboo Shoots](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000593)

### Works in this library

The [*Autobiography*](../works/zixu.md) held here is a cursive paper manuscript catalogued by the National Palace Museum, Taipei. The museum dates it to 777 and records a manuscript area of 28.3 by 755 cm. Li Dongyang and Gao Shiyi disagree in their colophons on whether the first six lines were supplied later; both opinions are preserved in the work record. [National Palace Museum, Tang Huaisu, Autobiography](https://digitalarchive.npm.gov.tw/Collection/Detail/14901?dep=P)

The Shanghai Museum's [*Letter on Bitter Bamboo Shoots*](../works/kusun.md) is catalogued as a Tang silk manuscript in cursive script, with a central writing area of 25.1 by 12 cm. A legacy museum page transcribes fourteen characters and records successive custody by the Song Xuanhe and Shaoxing palaces. Neither page gives an exact writing year. [Shanghai Museum collection entry](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00000593) · [Shanghai Museum legacy page](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/resource/winchance/n11.html)

### Works to copy

- [*Autobiography*](../works/zixu.md): Huaisu’s cursive account of his life and study of calligraphy, dated by the museum to 777.
- [*Letter on Bitter Bamboo Shoots*](../works/kusun.md): The Shanghai Museum’s Tang cursive letter by Huaisu on silk, with a central writing area of 25.1 by 12 cm; the precise writing year is unknown.

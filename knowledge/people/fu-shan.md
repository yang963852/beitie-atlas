# 傅山

## 中文

### 生平与身份

傅山（1607—1684），字青竹，后改青主，一字仁仲，别署公之它（一作他），号朱衣道人等，阳曲（今山西太原）人。明亡后隐居不仕，以行医为生。清康熙十七年（1678）举博学鸿词科，强征至京，以死拒，放归。能绘画，精医学；有《霜红龛集》行世，《清史稿》列传二百八十八有传。[故宫博物院，傅山小传](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228215.html)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于清。

### 书体与师承

故宫小传称傅山书法篆、隶、真、行、草无所不精，以草书最富特色，笔力雄奇恣肆，追求生拙真率的艺术境界，是清初书坛大家。所引小传未载其具体师承。[故宫博物院，傅山小传](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228215.html)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收[《草书孟浩然诗卷》](../works/fu-menghaoran.md)为傅山书写的孟浩然诗十八首，纸本，纵28.2厘米，横394.8厘米，今藏故宫博物院，文物号新00068982。卷末款署「山」，钤「傅山私印」，具体书写时间不详。故宫将此卷视为傅山草书上乘之作，并以行草书评价其代表性。[故宫博物院，藏品著录与简介](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228215.html)

## English

### Life and identity

Fu Shan (1607–1684), courtesy name Qingzhu (青竹), later changed to Qingzhu (青主), also known by the courtesy name Renzhong, used the style name Gongzhita (also given as Ta), and called himself Zhuyi daoren, among other names. He was a native of Yangqu, present-day Taiyuan in Shanxi. After the Ming fell, he lived in retirement without office and supported himself by practicing medicine. In 1678, the seventeenth year of the Kangxi reign, he was recommended for the examination for erudite literati, forcibly taken to the capital, refused by threatening death, and released. He could paint and was accomplished in medicine. His collected writings, the Shuanghongkan ji, circulated, and the Qing shi gao includes his biography in biography 288. [Palace Museum, biography of Fu Shan](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228215.html). By `dynastySource`, this page places him in the Qing period.

### Scripts and models

The Palace Museum biography says that Fu Shan mastered seal, clerical, regular, running and cursive scripts, with cursive as his most distinctive, describing his brushwork as vigorous, exuberant and unrestrained and his artistic aim as awkward, plain and spontaneous. It calls him a leading calligrapher of the early Qing. The cited biography does not name his teachers. [Palace Museum, biography of Fu Shan](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228215.html)

### Works in this library

The library’s [Scroll of Meng Haoran’s Poems in Cursive Script](../works/fu-menghaoran.md) transcribes eighteen poems by Meng Haoran in Fu Shan’s calligraphy, in ink on paper, 28.2 cm high and 394.8 cm long. It is held by the Palace Museum under accession 新00068982. The scroll is signed 山 and bears the seal 傅山私印; its exact writing date is unknown. The museum regards it as an outstanding example of Fu’s cursive and evaluates its representative importance in running-cursive terms. [Palace Museum, catalogue description and introduction](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228215.html)

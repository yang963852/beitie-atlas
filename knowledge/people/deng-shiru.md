# 邓石如

## 中文

### 生平与身份

邓石如（1743—1805），初名琰，因避清仁宗讳以字行，更字顽伯，号完白山人、笈游道人等，怀宁（今安徽安庆）人。少好刻石，弱冠谒江宁梅镠，梅家多藏金石善本，尽出与观，使专摹习，客梅氏八年；又客两江总督毕沅幕府，三年后辞归。书工各体，尤长于篆、隶；著《完白山人篆刻偶存》等，《清史稿》有传。[故宫博物院，邓琰隶书新洲诗轴](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228210.html)。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于清。

### 书体与师承

故宫记其书工各体，篆、隶颇得古法，兼融各家之长而自成风格，又记其隶书「以汉人为宗，汲取篆书笔意，再将魏碑方整厚重的特点融入隶书当中」；上博记其「工书法，擅篆刻，篆隶尤精」。[故宫博物院，邓琰隶书新洲诗轴](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228210.html) · [上海博物馆，清邓石如篆书谦卦轴](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00005003)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收[《楷书诗轴》](../works/deng-kaishu.md)为纸本墨迹；上博另藏《篆书谦卦轴》，书于乾隆四十六年（1781），时年三十九，洪福楣捐赠。[上海博物馆，清邓石如篆书谦卦轴](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00005003)

## English

### Life and identity

Deng Shiru (1743–1805), originally named Yan, took his courtesy name as his name to avoid the Qing emperor Renzong's taboo, later styled Wanbo, and called himself Wanbai shanren and Jiyou daoren; he was a native of Huaining, present-day Anqing in Anhui. He loved carving stone from youth; in his twenties he visited Mei Liu at Jiangning, whose family held many fine epigraphic books and let him study them exclusively for eight years; he later stayed three years in the household of Bi Yuan, governor-general of Liangjiang, then resigned and returned home. He wrote every script, most finely seal and clerical; his works include the Wanbai shanren seal-cutting collection, and the Qing shi gao carries his biography. [Palace Museum, Deng Yan's clerical-script Xinzhou poem](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228210.html). By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Qing.

### Scripts and models

The Palace Museum records that he "wrote every script, most finely seal and clerical, deeply versed in the old methods and fusing the strengths of several schools into a manner of his own," and that his clerical script "took the Han as its ancestor, drew on seal-script brushwork and then brought in the square, weighty qualities of the Wei steles." The Shanghai Museum says he "was skilled at calligraphy and seal carving, especially seal and clerical." [Palace Museum, Xinzhou poem](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228210.html) · [Shanghai Museum, Qian hexagram scroll](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00005003)

### Works in this library

The [regular-script poem scroll](../works/deng-kaishu.md) held here is ink on paper; the Shanghai Museum also holds a seal-script Qian hexagram scroll written in 1781 at thirty-nine, a gift of Hong Fumei. [Shanghai Museum, Qian hexagram scroll](https://www.shanghaimuseum.net/mu/frontend/pg/article/id/CI00005003)

# 鲜于枢

## 中文

《祭侄文稿》卷后鲜于枢两跋都记从曹彦礼处得卷，但丙戌署款跋记壬午（1282），戊子署款跋记癸未（1283）。两说并列，不合并为一个确定年份，也不据此补出交接地点。

### 生平与身份

鲜于枢（1246—1301），字伯几，号困学民、虎林隐吏、直寄道人、西溪翁。故宫博物院人物小传记其“自署渔阳（今北京市）人”，寓居扬州，后至杭州；历任湖南司宪经历、行台掾、浙江帅府从事、三司史掾等职，官至太常寺典簿，未到任便去世，终年56岁。这里保留馆方“自署”及今地措辞，不补写籍贯沿革。本卷末署「困學民書」，与小传相合。据 `dynastySource`，本页把他系于元。[故宫博物院，鲜于枢行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

### 书体与师承

故宫人物小传记鲜于枢“工于书法，尤善行草，取法唐人”，并称其在元代与赵孟頫齐名。本库《行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷》即馆方所定的行书大字作品；小传未列具体师承，不从“取法唐人”补出师承谱系。[故宫博物院，鲜于枢行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

### 代表作与本库关联

本库所收的[《祭侄文稿》](../works/jizhi.md)卷后存鲜于枢两跋。两跋都记从曹彦礼处得卷，年份作 1282 与 1283 两说，前文已并列存录；本页不据此补出交接地点或合并年份。

本库另收[《行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷》](../works/xianyu-zhaoling.md)，所书为杜甫五言《行次昭陵诗》。纸本手卷纵32厘米、横342厘米，故宫藏文物号新00145380；杜甫为诗作者，鲜于枢为书写者。馆方只定元代，未记书写年份，故不以1246—1301的生卒范围代替制作年代。[故宫博物院，鲜于枢行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)


### 可临之作

- [《祭侄文稿》](../works/jizhi.md)：颜真卿的行书《祭侄文稿》卷，鲜于枢1284年在杭州重装此卷。
- [《行书杜工部行次昭陵诗卷》](../works/xianyu-zhaoling.md)：鲜于枢以行书大字书写杜甫《行次昭陵诗》，故宫博物院藏纸本手卷；确切书写年份待考。

## English

Two colophons by Xianyu Shu on the Draft Requiem both say that he received the scroll from Cao Yanli, but the bingxu colophon gives 1282 and the wuzi colophon gives 1283. The project keeps both readings and does not supply a place of transfer.

### Life and identity

Xianyu Shu (1246–1301), courtesy name Boji, used the sobriquets Kunxue Min, Hulin Yinli, Zhiji Daoren and Xixi Weng. The Palace Museum biography says that he “signed himself as a native of Yuyang (present-day Beijing)”, lived in Yangzhou and later went to Hangzhou. It lists posts including Hunan judicial inspector, branch-secretariat clerk and Zhejiang military-office clerk; he was finally appointed secretary in the Court of Imperial Sacrifices but died before taking up the post, aged fifty-six. The wording preserves the museum’s “signed himself as” and its modern place-name without adding a history of his ancestry. The scroll’s final inscription “Kunxue Min writes” agrees with the biography. By `dynastySource` this page places him in the Yuan.[Palace Museum, Xianyu Shu’s running-script scroll of Du Fu’s poem on stopping at Zhaoling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

### Scripts and models

The Palace Museum biography says that Xianyu was “skilled in calligraphy, especially running and cursive script, taking Tang writers as models”, and that he ranked alongside Zhao Mengfu in the Yuan. The library’s *Running-Script Scroll of Du Fu’s Poem on Stopping at Zhaoling* is catalogued by the museum as large-character running script. The biography names no individual teachers, so no lineage is inferred from “taking Tang writers as models”.[Palace Museum, Xianyu Shu’s running-script scroll of Du Fu’s poem on stopping at Zhaoling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

### Works in this library

The library holds the [Draft Requiem for a Nephew](../works/jizhi.md) with two colophons by Xianyu Shu at its end. Both say he received the scroll from Cao Yanli, with the years given as 1282 and 1283; both readings are kept above, and this page supplies neither a merged year nor a place of transfer.

It also holds [*Du Fu’s Poem on Stopping at Zhaoling in Running Script*](../works/xianyu-zhaoling.md), written out by Xianyu from Du Fu’s five-character poem. The paper handscroll is 32 cm high and 342 cm long and is held by the Palace Museum under 新00145380. Du Fu wrote the poem and Xianyu wrote the calligraphy. The museum dates the object only to the Yuan and gives no writing year, so Xianyu’s lifespan of 1246–1301 is not used as a production range.[Palace Museum, Xianyu Shu’s running-script scroll of Du Fu’s poem on stopping at Zhaoling](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/228327.html)

### Works to copy

- [Draft Requiem for a Nephew](../works/jizhi.md): Yan Zhenqing’s running-script scroll *Draft Requiem for a Nephew*, remounted by Xianyu Shu at Hangzhou in 1284.
- [*Du Fu’s Poem on Stopping at Zhaoling in Running Script*](../works/xianyu-zhaoling.md): Xianyu Shu’s large-character running-script transcription of Du Fu’s poem, a paper handscroll at the Palace Museum whose exact writing year remains open.

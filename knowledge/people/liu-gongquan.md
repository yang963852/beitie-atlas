# 柳公权

## 中文

柳公权（778—865），字诚悬，京兆华原（今陕西耀县）人。宪宗元和初年进士，历中书舍人、谏议大夫、太子宾客，官至太子少师，世称“柳少师”，封河东郡公。故宫的小传称他“师颜真卿、欧阳询而能参化其神理，自成一局”，与颜真卿并称“颜筋柳骨”；又说穆宗曾问用笔之法，他答“用笔在心，心正则笔正”，语意双关，既论写字，也是笔谏。这句话可以当读柳书的入口：他讲的是骨力，不是姿态。[故宫博物院，柳公权行书蒙诏帖卷·柳公权](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/231095.html)

小传称他的楷书以《玄秘塔碑》《神策军碑》等最具特色，两件都在本库，刻石相隔两年。[《玄秘塔碑》](../works/xuanmi-ta-bei.md)立于会昌元年（841），裴休撰文，柳公权书丹并篆额，邵建和、邵建初兄弟刻石；原石今在西安碑林，故宫另藏一册明初拓本。[《神策军碑》](../works/shen-ce-jun-bei.md)立于会昌三年（843），崔铉撰文；原石在禁中，传拓不易，后来石佚，今日所见只是一卷宋装的上册，二十七开，经贾似道递藏，1965年归藏，今在中国国家图书馆。[西安碑林博物馆，玄秘塔碑](https://beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=148) · [中国文化报，宋拓《神策军碑》](https://nepaper.ccdy.cn/html/2012-06/22/content_74528.htm)

同一书手的两件代表作，一件有石、一件只剩纸。读玄秘塔要先把原石与拓册分开记账，到了神策军，拓本已是唯一的实物，石面的状态无从校对。故宫形容玄秘塔“用笔锐利，结体紧密，筋骨外露”，又说它“横画右肩稍高，略带欹侧之势”，见出“柳书的险绝”，推为“柳书最佳之临习范本”。这些话是对着原石说的，拿它去量拓本，才不至于把纸上的墨色当成石上的刀口。[故宫博物院，明初拓大达法师玄秘塔碑册](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/259370.html)

比这两碑更早的一方，反而最晚被人读到。[《迴元观钟楼铭》](../works/huiyuan-guan-zhonglou-ming.md)刻于开成元年（836），令狐楚撰文，柳公权书丹，石面一百二十三乘六十厘米，四十一行。此石历代金石书未见著录，1986年才在西安太乙路出土。它的好处正在“没被读过”：没有宋以来的翻刻，也很少有拓本递藏的题跋可依，字口离石面更近。看熟了名碑的拓本，不妨先在这块出土晚的石上认一遍骨力，再回头读玄秘塔。[西安碑林博物馆，迴元观钟楼铭](https://beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2581)

墨迹只有一卷，而且靠不住。[《蒙诏帖》](../works/mengzhao.md)纸本行书，纵二十六点八厘米、横五十七点四厘米，旧题柳公权，故宫定为非柳书。理由分两路。文辞上，帖中“出守翰林”不合唐人居官的称谓，南宋《兰亭续帖》所收《翰林帖》文字更顺，此卷或是宋人依其大意写出。笔法上，前四行用笔尚雄畅，后几行渐瘦薄、结字松散，末行“权”字笔画不成结构，与柳氏“遒媚劲健”的书风悬殊。这一卷读的不是柳公权，而是“旧题”怎么被拆掉：官称对不上、笔法前后不匀，两条都成立，归名便保不住。[故宫博物院，柳公权行书蒙诏帖卷](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/231095.html)

还有一块石，他只写了碑额。[《圭峰定慧禅师碑》](../works/guifeng.md)立于大中九年（855），裴休撰文并书丹，柳公权篆额，邵建初刻石。与玄秘塔并看，相隔十四年，同一组人合作了两次，角色却换了：玄秘塔是裴休撰、柳书丹，圭峰碑是裴休自书、柳篆额。碑额是另一套笔法，篆书与楷书不能互证。故宫所藏明拓中，“同中书”的“同”字尚未损坏，一字完缺即可辨本。[故宫博物院，圭峰定慧禅师碑](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234182.html)

### 可临之作

- [《玄秘塔碑》](../works/xuanmi-ta-bei.md)：柳公权841年书丹、裴休撰文的楷书碑，石藏西安碑林，另有明初拓册。
- [《神策军碑》](../works/shen-ce-jun-bei.md)：柳公权843年书的楷书碑，原石后佚，今见宋装拓本。
- [《迴元观钟楼铭》](../works/huiyuan-guan-zhonglou-ming.md)：柳公权晚年楷书横石，1986年西安出土，记迴元观钟楼旧事。
- [《蒙诏帖》](../works/mengzhao.md)：旧题柳公权的行书信札，故宫指出官称与文辞有疑，或为宋人依原意写成。
- [《圭峰定慧禅师碑》](../works/guifeng.md)：柳公权的篆书碑额见于裴休撰文的唐碑，故宫藏明拓本中「同」字尚未损坏。

## English

Liu Gongquan (778–865), courtesy name Chengxuan, was a native of Huayuan in the capital prefecture, present-day Yaoxian in Shaanxi. He passed the metropolitan examination in the early Yuanhe years and served as Secretariat drafter, remonstrance official and guest of the crown prince before rising to Junior Preceptor of the Heir Apparent, from which he was known as Young Preceptor Liu, and was enfeoffed as Duke of Hedong. The Palace Museum’s short biography says he “studied Yan Zhenqing and Ouyang Xun and transformed their spirit, forming a manner of his own”, and pairs him with Yan as “Yan’s sinew, Liu’s bone”. It also records that when Emperor Muzong asked him about brush method, he answered that the brush lies in the mind, and when the mind is upright the brush is upright — a double sense that spoke of writing and of remonstrance at once. That sentence is as good an entrance as any: what he writes is bone, not posture. [Palace Museum, Liu Gongquan’s Mengzhao letter, biography of Liu Gongquan](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/231095.html)

The same biography says that among his regular-script works the [Xuanmi Pagoda Stele](../works/xuanmi-ta-bei.md) and the [Shence Army Stele](../works/shen-ce-jun-bei.md) are the most distinctive. Both are in this library, carved two years apart. The Xuanmi stele was erected in 841, composed by Pei Xiu, written and given a seal-script heading by Liu, and cut by the brothers Shao Jianhe and Shao Jianchu; the stone is at the Xi’an Beilin Museum, and the Palace Museum holds a separate early Ming rubbing album. The Shence stele of 843 was composed by Cui Xuan and stood inside the palace compound, where rubbings were hard to take; the stone was later lost, and what survives is one volume of a Song mounting, twenty-seven openings, once in Jia Sidao’s collection and returned in 1965, now in the National Library of China. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Xuanmi stele](https://beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=148) · [China Culture Daily, Song rubbing of the Shence stele](https://nepaper.ccdy.cn/html/2012-06/22/content_74528.htm)

Two representative works by one hand, one stone and one sheet of paper. With the Xuanmi stele, keep the stone and the album in separate accounts; with the Shence stele the rubbing is all that is left, and the surface cannot be checked at all. The Palace Museum describes the Xuanmi stele as “cut with a sharp edge, tightly knit in structure, sinew showing on the outside”, its horizontal strokes “rising a little at the right shoulder, with a slight tilt”, which it reads as the “dangerous steepness” of Liu’s script, and calls the stele the best model for copying his work. Those words were written against the stone; use them to measure a rubbing and the ink will not be mistaken for the blade. [Palace Museum, early Ming rubbing of the Xuanmi stele](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/259370.html)

Earlier than either stele, this slab was the last to be read. The [Huiyuan Abbey Bell Tower Inscription](../works/huiyuan-guan-zhonglou-ming.md) was carved in 836, composed by Linghu Chu and written by Liu, on a slab 123 by 60 cm with forty-one lines. No epigraphic catalogue recorded it, and it came to light only in 1986 on Taiyi Road in Xi’an. Its virtue is exactly that it went unread: no Song recarving, few colophons of ownership to lean on, and letterforms that sit closer to the stone. Having looked long at famous rubbings, one might first take the measure of the bone on this late-found slab, then go back to the Xuanmi stele. [Xi’an Beilin Museum, Huiyuan inscription](https://beilin-museum.com/index.php?m=home&c=View&a=index&aid=2581)

Only one sheet of ink survives, and it will not hold. The [Mengzhao letter](../works/mengzhao.md) is ink on paper, running script, 26.8 by 57.4 cm, long attributed to Liu and judged by the Palace Museum not to be his. The argument runs two ways. In wording, “sent out to serve in the Hanlin” does not fit the titles of a Tang officeholder, and the Hanlin letter preserved in the Southern Song Lanting xu tie reads more naturally; this scroll may be a Song writer’s expansion of its sense. In brushwork, the first four lines are still vigorous, the later lines thin and loosely built, and the final “quan” of his signature falls apart as a character — far from the “supple, firm and strong” manner of Liu. What this scroll teaches is not Liu Gongquan but how an old attribution is taken apart: a title that does not fit and a hand that does not hold steady. With both in place, the name cannot stand. [Palace Museum, Liu Gongquan’s Mengzhao letter](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/handwriting/231095.html)

On one stone he wrote only the heading. The [Guifeng Chan Master Stele](../works/guifeng.md) was erected in 855, composed and written by Pei Xiu, with its seal-script heading by Liu and the cutting by Shao Jianchu. Set beside the Xuanmi stele, fourteen years apart, the same group worked twice with the roles exchanged: at Xuanmi, Pei composed and Liu wrote; at Guifeng, Pei wrote his own text and Liu supplied only the heading. A heading is another script altogether, and seal cannot vouch for regular. In the Palace Museum’s Ming rubbing the “tong” of “tong zhongshu” is still intact, and the soundness of a single character is enough to tell one impression from another. [Palace Museum, Guifeng stele](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/impres/234182.html)

### Works in this library

The library holds the [Xuanmi Pagoda Stele](../works/xuanmi-ta-bei.md), [Shence Army Stele](../works/shen-ce-jun-bei.md), [Huiyuan Abbey Bell Tower Inscription](../works/huiyuan-guan-zhonglou-ming.md), [Mengzhao letter](../works/mengzhao.md) and [Guifeng Chan Master Stele](../works/guifeng.md). ### Works to copy

- [Xuanmi Pagoda Stele](../works/xuanmi-ta-bei.md): A regular-script stele written by Liu Gongquan with text by Pei Xiu in 841, with its stone at the Xi’an Beilin and a separate early-Ming rubbing album in the Palace Museum.
- [Stele of the Shence Army](../works/shen-ce-jun-bei.md): A regular-script stele written by Liu Gongquan in 843, whose stone was later lost and survives through a Song-mounted rubbing.
- [Bell Tower Inscription of the Huiyuan Abbey](../works/huiyuan-guan-zhonglou-ming.md): A horizontal regular-script stone by Liu Gongquan in his late years, unearthed at Xi’an in 1986 and recording the Huiyuan Abbey’s bell tower.
- [Imperial Summons Letter](../works/mengzhao.md): A running-script letter traditionally attributed to Liu Gongquan, whose official title and wording lead the Palace Museum to suggest a Song writer working from the earlier text.
- [Stele of Chan Master Guifeng Dinghui](../works/guifeng.md): Liu Gongquan’s seal-script heading belongs to a Tang stele with text by Pei Xiu, and the character 同 remains undamaged in the Palace Museum’s Ming rubbing.
